# 📌 Active and Passive Voices of Perception Verbs
**TOEIC Grammar | Unit 11 — Active & Passive Voice | Part 3 of 4**
*Level: B1–B2 | Focus: Perception/Feeling Verbs — Subject Causes vs. Subject Receives*

---

## 🧭 Overview

> **Perception verbs** (also called emotive/feeling verbs) describe an emotional or sensory reaction.
> They are unique because the same verb can appear in both active AND passive voice — but with **different subjects**.
> - **Active:** The subject **causes** the feeling in someone else.
> - **Passive:** The subject **has/receives** the feeling themselves.

---

## 📐 Core Decision Rule

```
Subject CAUSES the feeling  →  Active Voice   (V + object)
Subject RECEIVES/HAS the feeling  →  Passive Voice (be + past participle)
```

## 📋 Active vs. Passive — Side-by-Side Comparison

| Active (Subject causes) | Passive (Subject receives) |
|------------------------|--------------------------|
| *"The results **pleased** both parties."* | *"I **am pleased** to hear that."* |
| *"The director **dissatisfied** the workers."* | *"The workers **are dissatisfied** with management."* |
| *"The news **surprised** the man."* | *"The man **was surprised** by the news."* |
| *"The manager **encouraged** her staff."* | *"The staff **were encouraged** by the manager."* |

---

## 🎯 Common TOEIC Traps

- **Trap 1 — `-ing` vs. `-ed` forms:** `disappointing` (active, describes the thing causing disappointment) vs. `disappointed` (passive, describes the person feeling it). *"The result was **disappointing**"* ≠ *"He was **disappointed**"*
- **Trap 2 — Noun form in feeling slot:** `disappointment` is a noun — cannot be used as a complement after `being` or `was`
- **Trap 3 — Active when subject feels:** *"The businessmen should guard against being **disappeared**"* ❌ → `disappointed` ✅ (past participle, passive form)
- **Trap 4 — `fascinating` vs. `fascinated`:** Subject = person feeling → `fascinated`. Subject = thing causing feeling → `fascinating`

---

## 🧠 Exam Thinking Strategy

> **Step 1:** Look at the **subject** of the sentence.
> **Step 2:** Does the subject **DO** something to cause a feeling in others?
> → **Active voice** → conjugated verb (`pleased`, `fascinated`, `encouraged`)
>
> **Step 3:** Does the subject **FEEL** or **RECEIVE** the emotion?
> → **Passive voice** → `be + past participle` (`was pleased`, `was fascinated`, `was encouraged`)
>
> **Step 4:** Check the options — eliminate:
> - Nouns (`disappointment`, `fascination`) — cannot be complements after `be`
> - `-ing` forms without `be` — not passive
> - Bare infinitives — not possible after `be + [blank]`

## 📝 Practice Questions

### Q1
> *"Young businessmen who set up venture companies should guard against being _____ during the first year of operations."*

- (A) disappoint · **(B) disappointed ✅** · (C) disappointing · (D) disappointment

> **Why (B) is correct:** The subject `businessmen` are the ones **feeling** the disappointment, not causing it in others. They receive the feeling → **passive voice** = `be + past participle`. The structure `being + past participle` = passive gerund → `being disappointed`. They should guard against experiencing disappointment.
>
> **Why (A) is wrong:** `disappoint` is the bare infinitive/base form. `being disappoint` is not grammatical — after `being`, you need a past participle for passive or an adjective, not a base verb.
>
> **Why (C) is wrong:** `disappointing` is the active present participle — it describes a **thing that causes** disappointment (e.g., *"the results were disappointing"*). Businessmen are not things causing disappointment; they are people who might experience it.
>
> **Why (D) is wrong:** `disappointment` is a **noun**. `being disappointment` is grammatically impossible — `being` here requires an adjective or past participle, not a noun.

---

### Q2
> *"When he took up cooking, Jerry Porter was _____ to learn how easy it was to make inexpensive, great-tasting meals."*

- (A) fascinate · (B) fascinating · (C) being fascinated · **(D) fascinated ✅**

> **Why (D) is correct:** Subject `Jerry Porter` is the one **feeling** fascination (he receives the feeling). The structure `was + past participle` = **passive** → `was fascinated`. This is a standard perception verb in passive voice, and `to learn...` is the infinitive phrase showing what fascinated him.
>
> **Why (A) is wrong:** `fascinate` is a bare infinitive. `was fascinate` is not grammatical — `was` requires either a past participle (passive), a present participle for progressive, or an adjective.
>
> **Why (B) is wrong:** `fascinating` is the active present participle — it means "causing fascination." `was fascinating` would make Jerry the one causing fascination in others (e.g., *"Jerry was fascinating to the audience"*), which contradicts the sentence context.
>
> **Why (C) is wrong:** `being fascinated` is a passive gerund — grammatically it could work in some contexts, but `was being fascinated` would mean the fascination was in progress at that exact past moment (past progressive passive), which sounds unnatural here. The simple `was fascinated` expresses his reaction correctly.

---

### Q3
> *"The manager _____ her staff with news of an upcoming bonus if sales figures reached company goals by the end of the quarter."*

- (A) encourage · (B) was encouraged · (C) encouraging · **(D) encouraged ✅**

> **Why (D) is correct:** The subject `the manager` is the one who **caused** the feeling of encouragement in `her staff`. Causing feeling → **active voice**. The sentence is in past tense, and there is an object `her staff` → `encouraged` (past simple, active) ✅.
>
> **Why (A) is wrong:** `encourage` is the base/present form. Past context + singular subject — if active and present, it would be `encourages`. `encourage` as a bare form here doesn't match the past-tense narrative of the sentence.
>
> **Why (B) is wrong:** `was encouraged` is **passive** (past) — this would make the manager the one *being* encouraged by her staff, which reverses the logical relationship. The manager is the encourager, not the encouraged one.
>
> **Why (C) is wrong:** `encouraging` is a present participle. `the manager encouraging her staff` is a participial phrase, not a main clause verb — the sentence has no main verb if you use this form. Or as a progressive: `was encouraging` (with `was`) could work, but the bare `encouraging` alone cannot serve as the main predicate.

## 🗂️ Error Log / Memory Tips

| Subject Role | Form | Example |
|-------------|------|---------|
| Causes feeling (= active) | Base verb / past simple | *"The news **surprised** the team."* |
| Receives feeling (= passive) | `be + past participle` | *"The team **was surprised** by the news."* |
| `-ed` = person/receiver | Adjective after `be` | *"She **was disappointed**."* |
| `-ing` = thing/cause | Adjective after `be` | *"The news **was disappointing**."* |
| Noun form | Never complement here | `disappointment` ≠ *"was disappointment"* |

> ✏️ **Quick Self-Test — Cover the right column:**
> - The results **[pleased / were pleased]** the investors. → `pleased` (results cause pleasure)
> - The investors **[pleased / were pleased]** with the results. → `were pleased` (investors feel pleasure)
> - Jerry **[fascinated / was fascinated]** by the cooking. → `was fascinated` (Jerry feels it)
> - The cooking demo **[fascinated / was fascinated]** the audience. → `fascinated` (demo causes it)
