# 📌 Expressions of the Passive Form
**TOEIC Grammar | Unit 11 — Active & Passive Voice | Part 4 of 4**
*Level: B1–B2 | Focus: `be + adj/p.p. + preposition` · `be + p.p. + to-infinitive` · Fixed Collocations*

---

## 🧭 Overview

> Beyond the basic passive (`be + past participle`), English has two families of **fixed passive expressions** that TOEIC tests as vocabulary + grammar combos:
> 1. **`be + adjective/past participle + preposition`** — fixed feelings and states
> 2. **`be + past participle + to-infinitive`** — fixed obligation/instruction structures
> You must know both the form **and** the correct preposition/particle that follows.

---

## 📂 Type 1 — `be + adjective/past participle + preposition`

> These are fixed expressions where the adjective/past participle is always followed by a **specific preposition**.
> Changing the preposition = error.

## 📂 Type 2 — `be + past participle + to-infinitive`

> These are passive expressions followed by a **to-infinitive** — used for instructions, permissions, obligations, and arrangements.
> The key test: blank must be a **past participle** (not bare infinitive, not `-ing`).

### High-Frequency List

| Expression | Meaning |
|------------|---------|
| `be asked to` | receive a request |
| `be told to` | receive an instruction |
| `be required to` | be obligated to |
| `be supposed to` | be expected to (arrangement/norm) |
| `be expected to` | be anticipated to |
| `be allowed to` | be given permission to |
| `be permitted to` | be given formal permission |
| `be requested to` | receive a formal request |
| `be urged to` | be strongly advised to |
| `be advised to` | receive advice |
| `be warned to` | receive a warning |
| `be reminded to` | receive a reminder |
| `be encouraged to` | be supported/motivated to |
| `be invited to` | receive an invitation |
| `be scheduled to` | be planned/arranged to |
| `be intended to` | be designed/aimed to |
| `be prepared to` | be ready to |

> 💡 **Examples:**
> - *"The project managers **are required to** fill in forms and get them signed."*
> - *"Employees **are reminded to** use office e-mail only for business purposes."*

## 🎯 Common TOEIC Traps

- **Trap 1 — Wrong preposition with `be dedicated`:** `be dedicated to` ✅ (not `for` or `with`)
- **Trap 2 — `-ing` after `be [p.p.] to`:** `"are invited attending"` ❌ → `are invited to attend` ✅ (always to-infinitive)
- **Trap 3 — Base verb instead of past participle:** `"is dedicate to"` ❌ → `is dedicated to` ✅
- **Trap 4 — Adverb instead of past participle:** `"are truly delightedly"` ❌ → `are truly delighted` ✅ (adjective/p.p., not adverb)
- **Trap 5 — Noun instead of past participle:** `"is dedication to"` ❌ → `is dedicated to` ✅

---

## 🧠 Exam Thinking Strategy

> **Step 1:** Is there a `be` verb already in the sentence? → Check what follows.
> **Step 2:** Does what follows = `be + [blank] + preposition`?
> → The blank needs a **past participle or adjective** that collocates with that preposition.
> **Step 3:** Does what follows = `be + [blank] + to + verb`?
> → The blank needs a **past participle** from the Type 2 list.
> **Step 4:** Eliminate:
> - Gerunds/`-ing` → not passive
> - Nouns → cannot be complements after `be` in this structure
> - Adverbs → wrong word class for this position
> - Base verbs → not past participle

## 📝 Practice Questions

### Q1
> *"The accounting team is _____ to providing accurate billing statements and resolving all customer enquiries within twenty-four hours."*

- (A) dedicating · **(B) dedicated ✅** · (C) dedicate · (D) dedication

> **Why (B) is correct:** The fixed expression is `be dedicated to` — `to` here is a **preposition**, not part of a to-infinitive. So what follows `to` is a gerund (`providing`). The blank must be the **past participle `dedicated`** to complete the passive expression.
>
> **Why (A) is wrong:** `dedicating` is the active present participle. `is dedicating to providing` would mean the team is actively in the process of dedicating something/someone else — reversed meaning and `is dedicating to` is not a standard expression.
>
> **Why (C) is wrong:** `dedicate` is the base/bare infinitive. `is dedicate to` is not grammatical. After `is`, you need either a past participle (passive), a present participle (progressive), or an adjective.
>
> **Why (D) is wrong:** `dedication` is a **noun**. `is dedication to` is grammatically impossible in this context — `be + noun` would need `a/the` (e.g., `is a dedication`), and even then changes the entire sentence structure and meaning.

---

### Q2
> *"As a member of our office, you are invited _____ the annual charity auction."*

- (A) attending · **(B) to attend ✅** · (C) attends · (D) attend

> **Why (B) is correct:** The fixed expression is `be invited to + [verb]`. Here, `to` is part of a **to-infinitive** (not a preposition). The blank requires `to attend` = to-infinitive following `are invited`.
>
> **Why (A) is wrong:** `attending` is a gerund/present participle. `are invited attending` is not grammatical — `be invited` requires a **to-infinitive**, not a gerund.
>
> **Why (C) is wrong:** `attends` is the third-person singular present conjugated verb. `you are invited attends` is grammatically impossible — you cannot place a conjugated verb directly after `are invited`.
>
> **Why (D) is wrong:** `attend` is the bare infinitive. `are invited attend` omits the required `to` — the correct passive expression is always `be invited **to** + base verb`. Missing `to` breaks the structure.

---

### Q3
> *"We are truly _____ with the positive consumer response to our new marketing strategy."*

- (A) delight · (B) delighting · **(C) delighted ✅** · (D) delightedly

> **Why (C) is correct:** The fixed expression is `be delighted with` — `delighted` is the **past participle** used as an adjective in passive form. Subject `we` receives/feels the delight. The structure: `are + [past participle] + with` = passive expression of feeling ✅.
>
> **Why (A) is wrong:** `delight` is a base verb or noun. `are delight with` is not grammatical. As a noun, `are delight with` is structurally broken. As a verb, `we delight with` changes meaning and voice.
>
> **Why (B) is wrong:** `delighting` is the active present participle. `are delighting with` would imply `we` are in the process of delighting someone else using something — reversed meaning, and `delight with` as active progressive is not standard usage.
>
> **Why (D) is wrong:** `delightedly` is an **adverb**. Adverbs cannot fill the slot after `are` in a `be + [complement]` structure. You cannot say `are delightedly` — an adjective or past participle is required here. `Delightedly` modifies verbs, not follows `be`.
