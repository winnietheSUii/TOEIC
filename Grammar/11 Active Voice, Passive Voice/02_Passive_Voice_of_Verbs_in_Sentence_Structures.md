# 📌 Passive Voice of Verbs in Sentence Structures
**TOEIC Grammar | Unit 11 — Active & Passive Voice | Part 2 of 4**
*Level: B1–B2 | Focus: Structure 4 (Two Objects) · Structure 5 (Object + Complement)*

---

## 🧭 Overview

> Some verbs take **two objects** or an **object + complement**. When these go passive, the structure shifts in a specific way.
> TOEIC tests whether you can recognize which element becomes the subject and what remains after the passive verb.

---

## 📂 Structure 4 — Ditransitive Verbs (Two Objects)

> Ditransitive verbs take both an **indirect object** (IO) and a **direct object** (DO).
> In passive voice, **either object can become the subject**.

### Active → Two Passive Options

| Voice | Sentence | Structure |
|-------|----------|-----------|
| **Active** | *"She gave **her sister** (IO) **the car** (DO)."* | Subject + V + IO + DO |
| **Passive 1** (IO as subject) | *"**Her sister** was given **the car**."* | IO becomes subject · DO stays after verb |
| **Passive 2** (DO as subject) | *"**The car** was given **to her sister**."* | DO becomes subject · IO takes `to` before it |

> 💡 When the **direct object** becomes the subject → the indirect object needs `to` in front of it.

---

## 📂 Structure 5 — Object + Object Complement

> Some verbs are followed by an **object** + **object complement** (noun or to-infinitive).
> In passive voice, the **object becomes the subject**, and the **complement stays** after the passive verb.

### Noun Phrase as Object Complement

| Voice | Sentence |
|-------|----------|
| **Active** | *"Victoria considered **him** (O) **a genius** (OC)."* |
| **Passive** | *"**He** was considered **a genius** by Victoria."* |

> → `him` = subject in passive → `a genius` stays after `was considered`. Don't mistake `a genius` for the object — it's a complement.

---

### To-Infinitive as Object Complement

| Voice | Sentence |
|-------|----------|
| **Active** | *"He asked **me** (O) **to send a stamped envelope** (OC)."* |
| **Passive** | *"**I** was asked **to send a stamped envelope**."* |

> → `me` = subject in passive → `to send a stamped envelope` stays intact after `was asked`.

---

## 🎯 Common TOEIC Traps

- **Trap 1 — Confusing complement for object:** After `be considered / be named / be called`, the noun following is a **complement**, not a second object. Don't expect it to disappear in passive.
- **Trap 2 — Forgetting `to` when DO becomes subject:** `"The car was given her sister"` ❌ → `"The car was given **to** her sister"` ✅
- **Trap 3 — Passive of `ask/remind/tell` + to-infinitive:** These keep the to-infinitive: `"I was asked **to send**"` ✅ (not `"I was asked send"`)
- **Trap 4 — Singular/plural agreement in passive:** `be` must agree with the new subject: `he` → `is/was`; `they` → `are/were`

---

## 🧠 Exam Thinking Strategy

> **Step 1:** Identify the verb type — does it take two objects? Or an object + complement?
> **Step 2:** Find what is now in the **subject** position — that's the original object that was promoted.
> **Step 3:** What follows the passive verb?
> - For ditransitive: the remaining object (with `to` if the DO was promoted)
> - For object complement: the complement stays (noun or to-infinitive)
> **Step 4:** Check: is the auxiliary `be` in the right form (singular/plural, right tense)?

## 📝 Practice Questions

### Q1
> *"Ray Murphy submitted a requisition form yesterday to make certain he _____ given a projector for today's presentation."*

- (A) has · (B) have · **(C) is ✅** · (D) are

> **Why (C) is correct:** The structure is `he [blank] given a projector` — a passive with ditransitive verb `give`. `he` (singular) is the indirect object promoted to subject. The auxiliary must agree: **singular `is`** → `he is given`. This is present tense (making certain something happens for today).
>
> **Why (A) is wrong:** `has` is a present perfect auxiliary — `he has given a projector` would make `he` the active giver, reversing the meaning entirely.
>
> **Why (B) is wrong:** `have` is plural. Subject `he` is singular, so `have` violates subject-verb agreement: `he have given` ❌.
>
> **Why (D) is wrong:** `are` is also plural. `he are` is grammatically impossible in English.

---

### Q2
> *"Though somewhat new at making presentations, Paul Blakely is the best public speaker of the team to _____ consider."*

- (A) consider · (B) to consider · (C) considering · **(D) considered ✅**

> **Why (D) is correct:** The full structure is `to be considered` (passive infinitive) — Paul is the one being evaluated/considered, not the one doing the considering. The `to be` is already implied/abbreviated, leaving `considered` (past participle) as the object complement in passive form. This is a **Structure 5** pattern.
>
> **Why (A) is wrong:** `consider` is the bare infinitive in active voice — it implies Paul does the considering of someone/something else, which contradicts the intended meaning.
>
> **Why (B) is wrong:** `to consider` is the active to-infinitive. Again, this means Paul considers others, which doesn't fit. The passive form would be `to be considered`.
>
> **Why (C) is wrong:** `considering` is a gerund/present participle. `is...to considering` is not a grammatical structure — you cannot combine `is` + `to` + `V-ing`.

---

### Q3
> *"The research team _____ to make back-up files of all their work to prevent loss of valuable data."*

- (A) reminded · **(B) was reminded ✅** · (C) will remind · (D) was reminding

> **Why (B) is correct:** `The research team` is the one **being reminded** by someone else (management, presumably). The team receives the action → **passive voice** → `was reminded`. The to-infinitive `to make back-up files` is the object complement that stays after the passive verb.
>
> **Why (A) is wrong:** `reminded` alone (past simple, active) would mean the team reminded someone else. But there's no object in the sentence to receive that reminder → grammatically incomplete and logically reversed.
>
> **Why (C) is wrong:** `will remind` is future active — making the team the one who will remind others in the future. This reverses the logical relationship and has no object.
>
> **Why (D) is wrong:** `was reminding` is past progressive active — the team was in the process of reminding someone else. Again, reversed meaning and no object.

## 🗂️ Error Log / Memory Tips

| Structure | Rule | Memory Trick |
|-----------|------|-------------|
| Ditransitive (IO as subject) | DO stays after verb | *"Her sister was given **the car**."* |
| Ditransitive (DO as subject) | IO gets `to` | *"The car was given **to** her sister."* |
| Object complement (noun) | Stays after passive verb | *"He was considered **a genius**."* |
| Object complement (to-inf) | Stays after passive verb | *"I was asked **to send**."* |
| Aux agreement | Match `be` to the new subject | `he → is/was` · `they → are/were` |
