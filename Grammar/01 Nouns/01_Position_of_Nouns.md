# 📌 Position of Nouns
**TOEIC Grammar | Unit 01 — Nouns | Part 1 of 6**
*Level: B1–B2 | Focus: Word Form & Sentence Structure*

---

## 🧭 Overview

> A **noun** is a word that names a person, place, thing, or idea.
> In a sentence, nouns can appear in **three key positions**.

---

## 📍 The Three Positions of a Noun

| Position | Role | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Subject** | Who/What does the action | *Customer satisfaction* must be considered. |
| **Object of a verb** | Receives the action | The library will accept *donations*. |
| **Object of a preposition** | After a preposition | Suncorp moved to its new *office*. |
| **Complement** | Describes the subject | Having a strong culture is an *advantage*. |

---

## 🔑 Where Nouns Appear in a Sentence

Nouns commonly follow these signal words:

- **Articles** → `a` / `an` / `the`
- **Adjectives** → a *successful* **meeting**
- **Possessive adjectives** → their **office**, our **team**
- **Prepositions** → at the **conference**, for **improvement**
- **Another noun** (compound) → *market* **research**

---

## ⚠️ The Core Rule

> **Only a noun can fill a noun position.**
> Verbs, adjectives, and adverbs are NOT acceptable substitutes.

### ❌ Common Errors

| Sentence | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| "...to encourage *invest* in small businesses." | `invest` is a **verb** | → `investment` (noun) |
| "...information regarding their *industrial*." | `industrial` is an **adjective** | → `industry` (noun) |

---

## 🧠 Exam Thinking Strategy

> **Step 1:** Find the blank in the sentence.
> **Step 2:** Look for a **signal word** before the blank (article, adjective, preposition).
> **Step 3:** If the blank is in a noun position, **eliminate all non-noun options immediately**.
> **Step 4:** Among noun options, select the one that makes logical sense.

---

## 🎯 Common TOEIC Traps

- **Trap 1 — Verb disguised as noun:** Options like `deliver` vs `delivery`. The presence of `the` before the blank = noun only.
- **Trap 2 — Adjective ending in -able:** `deliverable` *looks* like a noun but is usually an adjective.
- **Trap 3 — Past participle (verb-ed):** `evaluated` ends in `-ed` → still a verb form, not a noun.
- **Trap 4 — Two noun options:** e.g., `evaluator` (person) vs `evaluation` (process/result). Read the sentence for meaning.

---

## 📝 Practice Questions

### Q1
> *"I'd like to delay the _____ of the copy machine until we can move the old one into the basement."*

- (A) delivered
- (B) deliver
- (C) deliverable
- **(D) delivery ✅**

> 💡 Signal: `the` → noun required. `delivery` = the act of delivering.

---

### Q2
> *"...is expected to resume in time for the New Projects Committee's _____."*

- (A) evaluated
- **(B) evaluation ✅**
- (C) evaluator
- (D) evaluates

> 💡 Signal: Possessive `'s` → noun required. `evaluation` = the process of reviewing. `evaluator` = a person — wrong meaning here.

---

### Q3
> *"...are provided at great _____ to sickly and aging patients."*

- (A) convened
- (B) convenient
- (C) conveniently
- **(D) convenience ✅**

> 💡 Signal: `great` (adjective) → followed by noun. `at great convenience` is a fixed phrase.

---

## 🗂️ Error Log / Memory Tips

| Mistake | Root Cause | Remember This |
|---|---|---|
| Choosing a verb in noun position | Not checking for article/preposition signal | `the/a/an` = 100% noun follows |
| Choosing `-able` words | They look like nouns | `-able` = adjective (usually) |
| `evaluator` vs `evaluation` | Both are nouns | **-or/-er** = person; **-tion/-ment** = process/result |
| `convenient` vs `convenience` | Similar spelling | **-ent** = adjective; **-ence** = noun |

> ✏️ **Quick Formula:** `Article / Preposition / Adjective + [NOUN]`
