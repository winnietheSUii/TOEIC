# 📌 Common Nouns in TOEIC
**TOEIC Grammar | Unit 01 — Nouns | Part 6 of 6**
*Level: B1–B2 | Focus: Similar-Looking Nouns with Different Meanings*

---

## 🧭 Overview

> Many TOEIC traps involve **nouns that look or sound alike** but have completely different meanings.
> These are "decoy" options — designed to confuse test-takers who don't know the exact meaning.

---

## 📂 The Core Problem

> Words derived from the **same root** can take different noun forms — and each form has a **distinct meaning**.
> Choosing the wrong form = wrong answer, even if both are nouns.

### Example
> ❌ *"Pamela Hayes was given the **responsiveness** of preparing the equipment..."*
> ✅ *"Pamela Hayes was given the **responsibility** of preparing the equipment..."*
> → `responsiveness` = the quality of reacting quickly to something
> → `responsibility` = a duty or obligation assigned to someone

---

## 📋 Key TOEIC Noun Pairs — Meanings & Usage

| Noun A | Noun B | Key Distinction |
|---|---|---|
| **coverage** | cover / covering | `coverage` = scope of insurance/media; `cover` = a physical lid or protection |
| **delivery** | deliverance | `delivery` = bringing goods; `deliverance` = being saved/rescued (formal/religious) |
| **entry** | entrance | `entry` = a submission or act of entering (e.g., contest entry); `entrance` = the physical doorway |
| **identification** | identity | `identification` = the process of proving who you are; `identity` = who you are |
| **interests** | interest | `interests` (plural) = hobbies/stakes; `interest` (non-count) = curiosity or financial interest |
| **likelihood** | likeness | `likelihood` = probability; `likeness` = resemblance/similarity |
| **objective** | objectivity / objection | `objective` = goal; `objectivity` = being unbiased; `objection` = formal disagreement |
| **percent** | percentage | `percent` = used with a number (10 percent); `percentage` = a proportion/share |
| **permit** | permission | `permit` = a physical document/card; `permission` = an abstract right to do something |
| **procedure** | proceeding | `procedure` = a fixed method of doing something; `proceeding` = a legal or formal process |
| **production** | product / productivity | `production` = the process of making; `product` = the thing made; `productivity` = efficiency |
| **remainder** | remains | `remainder` = what is left (math/formal); `remains` = what is left after destruction, or a body |
| **responsibility** | responsiveness | `responsibility` = a duty; `responsiveness` = quickness to react |
| **segment** | segmentation | `segment` = a part/section; `segmentation` = the process of dividing |
| **sense** | sensation | `sense` = understanding or a faculty (taste, smell); `sensation` = a feeling or excitement |
| **utilization** | utility | `utilization` = the act of using something effectively; `utility` = usefulness or a public service |

## 🎯 Common TOEIC Traps

- **Trap 1 — `entry` vs `entrance`:**
  - `entry` = contest entry, journal entry, data entry (action/submission)
  - `entrance` = a door or opening you walk through
  - *"Any _____ submitted..."* → needs `entries` (submissions, not a doorway)

- **Trap 2 — `permit` vs `permission`:**
  - `permit` = a physical document (a parking permit, a work permit)
  - `permission` = an abstract approval ("ask for permission")
  - *"...obtaining a _____ from the attendant"* → `permit` (tangible document) ✅

- **Trap 3 — `sense` vs `sensation`:**
  - `sense` = practical understanding / a faculty ("a good business sense")
  - `sensation` = a physical or emotional feeling
  - *"a good business _____"* → `sense` ✅

- **Trap 4 — `percent` vs `percentage`:**
  - After a number → `percent`: *"10 percent discount"* ✅
  - Without a number → `percentage`: *"a large percentage of..."* ✅

---

## 🧠 Exam Thinking Strategy

> **Step 1:** Identify that two options are both nouns → this is a **meaning-based question**.
> **Step 2:** Read the full sentence carefully — skip nothing.
> **Step 3:** Ask:
> - "Does it refer to an **action/process** or a **physical object/document**?"
> - "Does it refer to a **quality/state** or a **duty/task**?"
> **Step 4:** Eliminate the decoy noun. Be precise — similar ≠ same.

## 📝 Practice Questions

### Q1
> *"Visitors are not allowed to use the back of the building to park their cars without obtaining a _____ from the attendant."*

- **(A) permit ✅**
- (B) permission
- (C) permitting
- (D) to permit

> 💡 "obtaining **a** _____" → singular count noun. A `permit` = a **physical card/document** you receive. `permission` = abstract, not preceded by `a` in this context.

---

### Q2
> *"Any _____ submitted without the requirements listed in the contest rules will automatically be disqualified."*

- (A) enter
- **(B) entries ✅**
- (C) entering
- (D) entrance

> 💡 "Any _____ submitted" → needs a noun that means *submissions to a contest*. `entries` = contest submissions. `entrance` = a physical doorway — wrong meaning entirely.

---

### Q3
> *"Having a good business _____ can help one to plan a career that involves long-term goals and strategies."*

- (A) to sense
- (B) sensing
- (C) sensation
- **(D) sense ✅**

> 💡 "a good business _____" → `sense` = practical understanding/judgment. `sensation` = a physical or emotional feeling — illogical in a business context.

## 🗂️ Error Log / Memory Tips

| Confusing Pair | Distinction | Memory Tip |
|---|---|---|
| `permit` vs `permission` | Document vs approval | A permit = card in your wallet; permission = words/approval |
| `entry` vs `entrance` | Submission vs doorway | "Entry fee" / "contest entries" = action; "entrance hall" = place |
| `responsibility` vs `responsiveness` | Duty vs reaction speed | `-bility` = capacity for something → your duty capacity |
| `sense` vs `sensation` | Judgment vs feeling | "Business sense" = fixed phrase; "a tingling sensation" = feeling |
| `percent` vs `percentage` | With number vs without | `10 percent` ✅ / `a percentage of...` ✅ |
| `likelihood` vs `likeness` | Probability vs similarity | "likelihood of success" = how likely; "likeness" = looks like |

> ✏️ **Final Strategy:** When you see two noun options that look similar → **slow down**. Read the sentence twice. Ask: "Which meaning fits the context: a task, an object, a process, or a quality?"
