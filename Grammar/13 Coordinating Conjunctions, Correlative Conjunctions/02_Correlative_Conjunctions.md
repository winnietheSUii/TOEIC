# 02_Correlative_Conjunctions

## 📌 Overview
**Correlative Conjunctions** = conjunction **pairs** that work together to join equal elements.

They always come in **fixed pairs** — you cannot mix them.
TOEIC tests: (1) correct pair matching, (2) subject-verb agreement, (3) parallel structure.

---

## 📊 Reference Table — All 5 Pairs

| Pair | Meaning | Example |
|------|---------|---------|
| **both A and B** | A and B (both included) | Both coffee and tea have long histories. |
| **either A or B** | one of A or B (choice) | Either bus or taxi is available from the airport. |
| **neither A nor B** | not A and not B (both excluded) | He is interested in neither reading nor writing. |
| **not A but B** | only B, not A (correction/replacement) | The office is not on the first floor but near the elevator. |
| **not only A but (also) B** | both A and B, with B emphasized | The shop not only offered discounts but also gave away souvenirs. |

---

## 🧠 Core Rules

### Rule 1 — Pairs are FIXED — cannot mix

```
either ... OR   ✅          either ... AND  ❌
neither ... NOR ✅          neither ... OR  ❌
not only ... BUT (also) ✅  not only ... AND ❌
both ... AND ✅             both ... OR  ❌
```

> ⚠️ **The #1 TOEIC trap:** "either A **and** B" → wrong → must be "either A **or** B"

### Rule 2 — Subject-Verb Agreement

| Pair | Verb agrees with... | Example |
|------|--------------------|---------||
| **both A and B** | always **PLURAL** | Both the manager and the staff **are** present. |
| **either A or B** | the **NEARER** subject (B) | Either the students or the teacher **is** responsible. |
| **neither A nor B** | the **NEARER** subject (B) | Neither the CEO nor the directors **were** informed. |
| **not only A but also B** | the **NEARER** subject (B) | Not only the staff but also the manager **was** surprised. |

```
Memory trick for OR / NOR / NOT ONLY:
→ "Proximity Rule" = verb follows the NEAREST subject
→ "both...and" is the exception = always plural
```

### Rule 3 — Parallel Structure (HIGH-YIELD TOEIC TRAP)

The elements joined by correlative conjunctions must be **grammatically parallel** — same word form.

```
✅ both NOUN and NOUN         → "both speed and accuracy"
✅ either VERB or VERB        → "either call or email"
✅ neither ADJ nor ADJ        → "neither fast nor reliable"
✅ not only CLAUSE but also CLAUSE

❌ "both speed and being accurate"     (noun ≠ gerund)
❌ "either call or to email"          (verb ≠ infinitive)
❌ "not only does A but B also..."    (check parallel structure)
```

### Rule 4 — "not only...but also" — Position Matters

```
Standard:  "She not only works hard but also inspires others."
Inverted:  "Not only does she work hard, but she also inspires others."
           (inverted structure when "not only" is at the front)

→ When "not only" starts the sentence → auxiliary verb comes before subject
```

## ⚠️ TOEIC Traps

| Trap | Wrong | Correct |
|------|-------|---------|
| Mixed pairs | either A **and** B | either A **or** B |
| Mixed pairs | neither A **or** B | neither A **nor** B |
| Mixed pairs | both A **or** B | both A **and** B |
| Agreement | Both options **is** good | Both options **are** good |
| Agreement | Either Mike or the staff **are** coming | Either Mike or the staff **is** coming (staff = singular) |
| Parallel | not only fast but also **reliably** | not only fast but also **reliable** (adj = adj) |
| "not...nor" | not A **or** B | not A **nor** B (formal) |

---

## 🎯 Exam Thinking Strategy

1. **See "either" in the sentence?** → blank must be **or**
2. **See "neither" in the sentence?** → blank must be **nor**
3. **See "not only" in the sentence?** → blank must be **but (also)**
4. **Two subjects with OR/NOR?** → verb agrees with **nearer** subject
5. **"both...and" with two subjects?** → always **plural** verb
6. **Check parallel structure:** what form is A? → B must be the same form
7. **"not...but"** = correction pattern → B replaces A

## ✏️ Practice Questions

**Q1.** You can purchase a copy of his novel either at a bookstore ___ through popular bookstore websites.
- (A) **or** ✅
- (B) nor
- (C) and
- (D) also

> **Why (A):** "Either...or" is a fixed pair. Blank must complete the pair.
> (B) "nor" pairs with "neither," not "either." (C) "and" pairs with "both," not "either." (D) "also" is an adverb, not a conjunction.

---

**Q2.** Information will be presented on global communication, but ___ on presentation technology and sales marketing.
- (A) for
- (B) **nor** ✅
- (C) so
- (D) not

> **Why (B):** "not...nor" = excludes both things. The sentence excludes global communication + presentation technology from the scope. "but not...nor..." is the exclusion structure.
> (A) "for" = reason. (C) "so" = result. (D) "not" alone cannot follow "but" in this context without "nor" to complete the pair.

---

**Q3.** The director complained that the report was ___ too lengthy but also too detailed.
- (A) only
- (B) not
- (C) **not only** ✅
- (D) either

> **Why (C):** "not only...but also" is a fixed pair. "...but also too detailed" is already in the sentence → the blank needs "not only" to complete the pair.
> (A) "only" alone doesn't pair with "but also." (B) "not...but also" is not the standard pair. (D) "either...but also" doesn't exist as a pair.
