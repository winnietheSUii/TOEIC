# 01_Coordinating_Conjunctions

## 📌 Overview
**Coordinating Conjunctions** = FANBOYS: **F**or, **A**nd, **N**or, **B**ut, **O**r, **Y**et, **S**o

They join **grammatically equal** elements:
- Word + Word: "fast **and** accurate"
- Phrase + Phrase: "in the office **or** at home"
- Clause + Clause: "He left, **but** she stayed."

TOEIC tests: (1) choosing the right meaning, (2) subject-verb agreement after AND/OR

---

## 📊 Reference Table — All 7 FANBOYS

| Conjunction | Meaning / Function | Example |
|-------------|-------------------|---------|
| **and** | Addition / connection | Eating should be healthful **and** enjoyable. |
| **or** | Alternative / choice | I will go camping **or** canoeing. |
| **but** | Contrast / exception | She likes jazz, **but** he prefers rock. |
| **yet** | Contrast (more formal than "but") | The offer is appealing, **yet** I must decline. |
| **so** | Result / consequence | It was raining, **so** we stayed inside. |
| **for** | Reason / cause (formal) | There will be a party, **for** he is retiring. |
| **nor** | Negative alternative | He didn't call, **nor** did he text. |

---

## 🧠 Core Rules

### Rule 1 — Choose by Meaning / Logic

| Relationship between two clauses | Use |
|----------------------------------|-----|
| Adding information | **and** |
| Choosing between options | **or** |
| Contrasting / unexpected result | **but** / **yet** |
| Giving a reason for what came before | **for** |
| Showing a result of what came before | **so** |
| Negative continuation | **nor** |

> ⚡ **Key difference: "for" vs "so"**
> - **for** = reason (explains why the first clause happened) → reads backwards
> - **so** = result (shows what happened because of the first clause) → reads forwards
>
> ✅ "It was cold, **so** we wore coats." (result)
> ✅ "We wore coats, **for** it was cold." (reason)

### Rule 2 — Subject-Verb Agreement with AND / OR

```
A AND B  → always PLURAL verb
A OR B   → verb agrees with the NEARER subject (B)

✅ "Mark and Scott ARE going to the meeting."    (and → plural)
✅ "I or our teacher IS going to solve it."       (nearest = teacher → singular)
✅ "Our teacher or I AM going to solve it."       (nearest = I → first person)
```

### Rule 3 — "yet" vs "but"
```
BUT = standard contrast (most common)
YET = formal contrast (same meaning, higher register — TOEIC loves this)

✅ "The plan is risky, but we will proceed." (neutral)
✅ "The plan is risky, yet we will proceed." (formal — Part 6 context)
```

### Rule 4 — "for" as conjunction vs preposition
```
"for" CONJUNCTION = reason (= because) — formal, before full clause
"for" PREPOSITION = purpose — before noun only

✅ "He retired, for he had worked 40 years." (conj = because)
✅ "A party for his retirement." (prep = purpose)
```

---

## ⚠️ TOEIC Traps

| Trap | Wrong | Correct |
|------|-------|---------|
| Result vs Reason | "It was cold, for we wore coats." | It was cold, **so** we wore coats. |
| Addition vs Alternative | "You can call either Mike **and** Sarah." | either Mike **or** Sarah |
| Agreement with OR | "Mark or Scott **are** responsible." | Mark or Scott **is** responsible (nearest = Scott = singular) |
| "yet" as adverb vs conjunction | "I haven't eaten yet" (adverb) | "He's tired, yet he works." (conjunction) |
| "so" vs "so that" | "She studied so she can pass." | She studied **so that** she could pass. (purpose requires "so that") |

---

## 🎯 Exam Thinking Strategy
1. **What's the relationship?** → addition (and) / contrast (but/yet) / result (so) / reason (for) / choice (or) / negative (nor)
2. **See "either"?** → must pair with **or**
3. **See "neither"?** → must pair with **nor**
4. **Two subjects joined by OR?** → verb agrees with the **closer** subject
5. **Two subjects joined by AND?** → always **plural** verb
6. **Formal register (Part 6)?** → consider **yet** instead of **but**, **for** instead of **because**

## ✏️ Practice Questions

**Q1.** The corporation's portfolio of clients consisted of representatives from 13 American states ___ 25 countries worldwide.
- (A) either
- (B) so
- (C) **and** ✅
- (D) too

> **Why (C):** Two things are being added together (13 states + 25 countries) → **and** for addition.
> (A) "either" needs "or" as a pair. (B) "so" = result, not addition. (D) "too" is an adverb, cannot join noun phrases.

---

**Q2.** Your promotion offer is very appealing, ___ I'm afraid that I can't sacrifice my family life for my career.
- (A) **so, but** ✅ ← (noting this is a two-blank question requiring "so" before appealing and "but" before "I'm afraid")
- (B) yet, though
- (C) besides
- (D) therefore

> **Why (A):** The first sentence presents a positive fact. The second sentence introduces a contrast (can't accept despite it being appealing) → **but** for contrast. The sentence structure requires coordinating conjunctions.
> (B) "though" is a subordinating conjunction, not coordinating. (C)(D) are adverbs, not conjunctions.

---

**Q3.** Mark Melendy ___ Scott Drake are going to represent Balmar Associates at the branch meeting.
- (A) but
- (B) **and** ✅
- (C) either
- (D) or

> **Why (B):** Two people are both attending — this is addition, not a choice → **and**. Also note: "Mark AND Scott" = plural → "are going" ✅ (plural verb matches).
> (A) "but" = contrast, illogical here. (C) "either" needs "or" as a pair. (D) "or" = choice between them, but both are going.
