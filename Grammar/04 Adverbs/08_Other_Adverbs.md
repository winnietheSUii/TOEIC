# 📌 Other Adverbs
**TOEIC Grammar | Unit 04 — Adverbs | Part 8 of 8**
*Level: B1–B2 | Focus: Also/Too/As Well/Either · Later/Thereafter/Since · Forward/Ahead*

---

## 🧭 Overview

> Some adverbs are tricky not because of meaning, but because of **position** in the sentence.
> This section covers three important groups that TOEIC frequently tests.

---

## 📂 Group 1 — Also, Too, As Well, Either

> These four all mean **"in addition"** — but their positions are completely different.

| Adverb | Position | Sentence Type | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **also** | Beginning or **middle** of sentence | Affirmative | *I have shares in Amsun and **also** Talba.* |
| **too** | **End** of sentence | Affirmative | *There will be workshops offered next month **too**.* |
| **as well** | **End** of sentence | Affirmative | *Mary wants to apply for the position **as well**.* |
| **either** | **End** of sentence | **Negative** | *Luis won't go; Pedro won't **either**.* |

> ⚠️ **`also` does NOT go at the end of a sentence.**
> ⚠️ **`either` is only used in negative contexts** — it's the negative equivalent of `too/as well`.

---

## 📂 Group 2 — Later, Thereafter, Since

> These three all deal with **time sequence**, but from different reference points.

### ⏩ Later
- Means: **"after the time mentioned"** or **"at some point in the future"**
- Common in: past context or future context

> *"He was questioned about the scandal five months **later**."* (past)
> *"Why don't we talk this over **later**?"* (future)

---

### 📋 Thereafter
- Means: **"from a specific point onward"** / **"after that"** (formal, written English)
- Used in **formal documents, announcements, policies**

> *"Sally left and her package was delivered shortly **thereafter**."*
> *"The plane will take off at 3:15 p.m., and a light snack will be served to passengers **thereafter**."*

---

### 🔗 Since
- Means: **"from a particular time in the past until now"** (used with present perfect)
- Often follows: `has/have + past participle + since`

> *"Anne Keating was promoted and has **since** worked as head accountant."*

---

## 📂 Group 3 — Forward vs. Ahead

> Both relate to movement or direction, but they have **distinct nuances**.

| Adverb | Core Meaning | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **ahead** | Direction / future time | Physical direction; future time → *the days **ahead*** |
| **forward** | State / continuation | Progress or continuation of an activity |

| Example | Which one & Why |
|---|---|
| *"The director is working hard to push the company **ahead**."* | `ahead` = direction of progress |
| *"Push something **forward** means to continue with an activity in a determined way."* | `forward` = continuing an ongoing activity |
| *"The days **ahead** would be tiresome."* | `ahead` = future time (upcoming days) |

---

## 🎯 Common TOEIC Traps

- **Trap 1 — `also` at the end:** "She applied for the position **also**." ❌ → Use `too` or `as well` at the end.
- **Trap 2 — `too` in negative context:** "She won't apply **too**." ❌ → Use `**either**." ✅
- **Trap 3 — `later` vs `thereafter`:** `later` = informal, any context; `thereafter` = formal, policies, official documents
- **Trap 4 — `ahead` vs `forward`:** "step **forward**" (take action/continue) vs "move **ahead**" (make progress in a direction) — context-dependent but `ahead` often pairs with directions

---

## 🧠 Exam Thinking Strategy

> **Step 1 (Also / Too / As Well / Either):**
> - Check sentence type: affirmative or negative?
> - Check blank position: beginning/middle vs. end?
> - Negative + end → `either`; Affirmative + end → `too` / `as well`; Middle → `also`

> **Step 2 (Later / Thereafter / Since):**
> - Is the register formal (policies, documents) → `thereafter`
> - Is there a reference to a past moment + current state → `since` (with present perfect)
> - General "at a later time" → `later`

> **Step 3 (Ahead / Forward):**
> - Physical direction or future time → `ahead`
> - Continuing an activity despite difficulty → `forward`

## 📝 Practice Questions

### Q1
> *"Ms. Peters thought the designs were marvelous, but she needs to see what they would look like in a display window _____."*

- (A) not only
- (B) either
- **(C) also ✅**
- (D) as well

> 💡 This sentence is **affirmative**. The blank is in the **middle** of the sentence (before a clause). `also` ✅ fits the mid-sentence position. `as well` goes only at the end. `either` = negative only.

---

### Q2
> *"The plane will take off at about 3:15 p.m., and a light snack will be served to the passengers _____."*

- (A) later
- **(B) thereafter ✅**
- (C) already
- (D) suddenly

> 💡 Context: official flight sequence (formal). "After the plane takes off, snacks will be served **thereafter**" = from that point onward. `later` is less formal and less precise in formal writing. `thereafter` = the formal sequencing word.

---

### Q3
> *"When Bernal became the CEO, we considered it a great step because a woman was finally guaranteed formal say in company policy _____."*

- **(A) ahead ✅**
- (B) forward
- (C) towards
- (D) backward

> 💡 "formal say in company policy **ahead**" = in the future / in the road ahead, moving the company in a positive direction. `ahead` refers to the **future direction** of the company.

## 🗂️ Error Log / Memory Tips

| Mistake | Fix | Memory Trick |
|---|---|---|
| `also` at end of sentence | Use `too` or `as well` | `also` = mid-sentence only |
| `too` in negative sentence | Use `either` | "won't... **either**" = negative `too` |
| `later` in formal documents | Use `thereafter` | `thereafter` = formal language for sequence |
| `forward` when direction needed | Use `ahead` | `ahead` = a direction; `forward` = continuation |

> ✏️ **Position Summary — "In Addition" Adverbs:**
>
> | Middle of sentence | End (affirmative) | End (negative) |
> |---|---|---|
> | `also` | `too` / `as well` | `either` |
