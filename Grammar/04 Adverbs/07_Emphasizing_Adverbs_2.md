# 📌 Emphasizing Adverbs (2)
**TOEIC Grammar | Unit 04 — Adverbs | Part 7 of 8**
*Level: B1–B2 | Focus: So / Such / Very / Too*

---

## 🧭 Overview

> `so`, `such`, `very`, and `too` all express **intensity** — but each has a different **grammatical target** and **meaning**.
> Getting these wrong is one of the most common TOEIC mistakes.

---

## 📂 So vs. Such

### 🔑 The Core Rule

| Word | Grammatical Function | Modifies | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **so** | Adverb | Adjective or Adverb | *The store was **so** busy.* |
| **such** | Determiner | Noun or Noun Phrase | *It was **such** a busy store.* |

> Both mean **"very"**, but they attach to **different word classes**.

---

### ✅ So — Adjective / Adverb

> Structure: `so + adjective` OR `so + adverb`

> *"The store was **so** busy that it had to hire part-time workers."*
> *"Sales items sold out **so** quickly that the shop had to close early."*

> Optional: `so + adj/adv + that-clause` → expresses a result

---

### ✅ Such — Noun / Noun Phrase

> Structure: `such + a/an + (adjective) + noun`

> *"It's amazing that one company can have **such** dedicated employees."*
> *"The chess championships are **such** popular events that the auditorium is always packed."*

---

### ❌ Common Errors

| Wrong | Right | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| *The store was **such** busy.* | *...was **so** busy.* | `busy` = adjective → use `so` |
| *one company can have **so** dedicated employees* | *...**such** dedicated employees* | `employees` = noun phrase → use `such` |

---

## 📂 So vs. Very vs. Too

| Word | Basic Meaning | Used with `that`-clause? | Implies problem? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **very** | Emphasizes a characteristic | ❌ No | Neutral |
| **so** | Emphasizes strongly | ✅ Yes (so...that) | Neutral or leading to result |
| **too** | Emphasizes excess | ❌ No (uses `to + verb`) | **Yes — negative implication** |

---

### ✅ Very — Neutral Emphasis

> *"The explanation in the book is **very** clear."* → Simple emphasis, no problem implied.
> *"The walk was **very** long, but the fresh air felt good."* → Long, but it's fine.

---

### ✅ So — Emphasis with Result

> *"The staff was **so** argumentative **that** it took time before decisions were made."*
> → Use `so` when followed by a `that`-clause explaining the result.

---

### ✅ Too — Negative Excess

> *"The report was **too** long **to** read all at once."*
> → `too` implies a **problem**: it's excessively long, so you can't read it fully.
> Structure: `too + adjective/adverb + to + verb`

---

### ✅ Much Too — Strong Excess

> *"Taxes are **much too** heavy for the smaller businesses to handle."*
> → `much too` = even more excessive → stronger negative than `too` alone.

---

## 🎯 Common TOEIC Traps

- **Trap 1 — `such` before adjective (no noun):** "such busy" ❌ → "so busy" ✅ — no noun follows = use `so`
- **Trap 2 — `so` before noun phrase:** "so dedicated employees" ❌ → "such dedicated employees" ✅
- **Trap 3 — `too` when meaning is positive:** "The director was **too** satisfied" ❌ (implies a problem) → use `very satisfied` ✅
- **Trap 4 — `very` with `that`-clause:** "The report was **very** long **that** we couldn't finish" ❌ → use `so` for result clauses

---

## 🧠 Exam Thinking Strategy

> **Step 1:** Look at what comes **after** the blank.
> - Adjective alone → **so** or **very**
> - Noun phrase (with or without adjective) → **such**
> **Step 2:** Check if there's a `that`-clause or `to + verb` after:
> - Result `that`-clause → **so**
> - Negative excess `to + verb` → **too**
> **Step 3:** Check the **tone** of the sentence:
> - Positive / neutral → **very** or **so**
> - Negative / problem → **too** or **much too**
> **Step 4:** `such` always needs a noun somewhere after it (even if an adjective comes between).

## 📝 Practice Questions

### Q1
> *"The chess championships at the school are _____ popular events that the auditorium is always packed on tournament days."*

- (A) so
- (B) huge
- **(C) such ✅**
- (D) too

> 💡 "_____ popular events" — `events` = noun → use `such`. Also note the `that`-clause follows: `such + noun + that` is a valid structure.
> `so popular events` ❌ — `so` can't modify a noun phrase directly.

---

### Q2
> *"Considering that the job was done in a rush, the director was _____ satisfied with the results."*

- (A) too
- (B) such
- (C) far
- **(D) very ✅**

> 💡 `satisfied` = adjective. Sentence tone = positive (pleased despite the rush) → neutral emphasis → `very`. `too satisfied` implies a problem ❌.

---

### Q3
> *"Relative to the average gross revenue, taxes in the area are _____ heavy for the smaller businesses to handle."*

- **(A) much too ✅**
- (B) such
- (C) so
- (D) much

> 💡 "heavy **for the businesses to handle**" = negative implication (a problem) → need `too`. `much too` strengthens it: extremely excessive. `so heavy` alone doesn't carry the `to-infinitive` structure that signals excess.

## 🗂️ Error Log / Memory Tips

| Mistake | Fix | Memory Trick |
|---|---|---|
| `such busy` | `so busy` | Adjective alone → `so` |
| `so dedicated employees` | `such dedicated employees` | Noun phrase follows → `such` |
| `very` with `that`-clause | `so` with `that`-clause | "so...that" = result structure |
| `too` in positive context | `very` in positive context | `too` = always a problem/excess |
| `too` without `to-infinitive` | Add `to + verb` | "too busy **to** focus" — complete the structure |

> ✏️ **Cheat Sheet:**
> - **so** → modifies adj/adv → can lead to `that`-clause
> - **such** → modifies noun phrase → "such a + noun"
> - **very** → neutral emphasis of adj/adv → no `that`-clause
> - **too** → excess, negative → leads to `to + verb`
> - **much too** → strongly negative excess
