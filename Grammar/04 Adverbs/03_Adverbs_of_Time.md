# 📌 Adverbs of Time
**TOEIC Grammar | Unit 04 — Adverbs | Part 3 of 8**
*Level: B1–B2 | Focus: Already / Still / Yet / Ever / Ago / Once*

---

## 🧭 Overview

> Adverbs of time tell us **when** or **for how long** something happens.
> TOEIC frequently tests `already`, `still`, `yet` — three words that look interchangeable but follow strict rules.

---

## 📂 Group 1 — Already, Still, Yet

### ✅ Already
- Used in **affirmative** sentences
- Signals something has been done **sooner than expected**
- Position: between auxiliary and main verb, or at end of sentence

> *"She has **already** typed the report you left on her desk."*
> *"The play had **already** started by the time we arrived."*

---

### 🔄 Still
- Used in **affirmative, negative, and interrogative** sentences
- Signals something is **continuing** (ongoing / not changed)
- Position: before the main verb, or between auxiliary and main verb

> *"The house is **still** up for sale."* (continuing situation)
> *"They are **still** arranging the office."*

---

### ❓ Yet
- Used in **negative and interrogative** sentences
- Signals something **has not happened but is expected to**
- Position: usually at the **end of the sentence**

> *"Have the reports been filed **yet**?"* (question)
> *"The administration has not devised a policy that would allow for it **yet**."* (negative)

---

## 🔑 Quick Comparison Table

| Adverb | Sentence Type | Key Meaning | Position |
|---|---|---|---|
| **already** | Affirmative | Done sooner than expected | Mid / End |
| **still** | All types | Continuing, ongoing | Mid (before verb) |
| **yet** | Negative / Question | Not happened, but expected | End |

---

## 📂 Group 2 — Ever, Ago, Once

### 🌀 Ever
- Used in **negative and interrogative** sentences
- Refers to **any time in the past** (no specific time)
- Common in: *hardly ever*, *have you ever*, *ever since*

> *"They **hardly ever** received encouragement from their parents."*
> *"Union leaders have been negotiating **ever since** the workers went on strike."*

---

### ⏱️ Ago
- Follows an **expression of time** to say how far before now something happened
- Structure: `[time expression] + ago`

> *"Mrs. Baker joined our team **three years ago**."*
> *"The workers went on strike about two weeks **ago**."*

---

### 1️⃣ Once
- As a time adverb: means **at a time in the past** (but not anymore)
- As a modifier before adjective: means **formerly**

> *"He **once** managed this store."* (in the past, not anymore)
> *"...many **once** thriving fish markets."* (formerly thriving)

---

## 🎯 Common TOEIC Traps

- **Trap 1 — `already` in a negative sentence:** "She already hasn't..." ❌ → Use `yet`: "She hasn't... **yet**."
- **Trap 2 — `yet` in an affirmative sentence:** "He has yet submitted the report." ❌ → Use `already`: "He has **already** submitted..."
- **Trap 3 — `still` vs `already`:** `still` = ongoing process; `already` = completed sooner than expected → Read for meaning
- **Trap 4 — `ever since` vs `already`:** "Ever since" = from a past point until now (continuous timeline). Don't confuse with `already` (single completed action).

---

## 🧠 Exam Thinking Strategy

> **Step 1:** Check the **sentence type** — affirmative, negative, or question?
> **Step 2 (Already / Still / Yet):**
> - Affirmative + completed = **already**
> - Ongoing / continuing = **still**
> - Negative / question + expected = **yet**
> **Step 3 (Ever / Ago / Once):** Look for time expressions or `hardly` → `ever since` for continuous past; `ago` after a duration.

## 📝 Practice Questions

### Q1
> *"...by the time we arrived at the theater, the award-winning play had _____ started."*

- (A) yet
- **(B) already ✅**
- (C) still
- (D) just

> 💡 Affirmative sentence. The play started **sooner than expected** (before they arrived) → `already`. `yet` = negative/question only.

---

### Q2
> *"...the administration has not devised a policy that would allow for it _____."*

- **(A) yet ✅**
- (B) still
- (C) already
- (D) subsequently

> 💡 Negative sentence (`has not`). Something is expected but hasn't happened → `yet`. Place it at the **end of the sentence**.

---

### Q3
> *"Union leaders have been negotiating with management representatives _____ since most of the company's workers went on strike about two weeks ago."*

- **(A) ever ✅**
- (B) already
- (C) yet
- (D) still

> 💡 "_____ since" = fixed phrase `ever since` = from a past point continuously until now. `already since` and `yet since` are not standard English.

## 🗂️ Error Log / Memory Tips

| Mistake | Fix | Memory Trick |
|---|---|---|
| `yet` in affirmative | Use `already` | `yet` only in negative/question |
| `already` in negative | Use `yet` | "not... yet" = expected but not done |
| `still` confused with `already` | `still` = still going; `already` = done | `still` = ongoing; `already` = finished early |
| `ever since` separated | Keep together | `ever since` = compound time phrase |

> ✏️ **Quick Formula:**
> - ✅ done (affirmative) → **already**
> - ⏳ ongoing → **still**
> - ❌ not yet / question → **yet**
> - Past→Now continuous → **ever since**
