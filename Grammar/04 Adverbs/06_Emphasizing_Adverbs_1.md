# 📌 Emphasizing Adverbs (1)
**TOEIC Grammar | Unit 04 — Adverbs | Part 6 of 8**
*Level: B1–B2 | Focus: Just / Right / Well / Even / Quite + Comparisons*

---

## 🧭 Overview

> **Emphasizing adverbs** add intensity, exclusivity, or precision to the words they modify.
> Each one has a specific grammatical partner — you must match the right emphasizer to the right structure.

---

## 📂 Group 1 — Just, Right, Well

### 🎯 Just & Right — Emphasizing Time Adverbs

> **Just** and **Right** come **before** `before` and `after` to emphasize timing.

| Example | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Get in touch **just before** coming to the office.* | Immediately before |
| *Contact us **right after** the meeting.* | Immediately after |

---

### 🎯 Only & Just — Emphasizing Prepositional or Noun Phrases

> **Only** and **Just** restrict meaning: "nothing more than / exclusively"

| Example | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *They hold barbecue parties **only** during the summer.* | Exclusively during summer |
| *It's **just** a simple misunderstanding.* | Nothing more than |

---

### 🎯 Well — Emphasizing Prepositional Phrases

> **Well** comes before a prepositional phrase to mean **"very much" / "by a large amount"**

| Example | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Stock prices are **well** over market value.* | Far over / significantly over |
| *The price was **well** below expectations.* | Far below |

> Common collocations: `well over` · `well below` · `well above` · `well beyond` · `well within`

---

## 📂 Group 2 — Even

> **Even** emphasizes something **surprising or unexpected**.
> - Before a **noun/noun phrase** → *Even the professor thought it was boring.*
> - Before a **verb** (between modal and main verb) → *We will **even** provide complimentary mints.*

---

## 📂 Group 3 — Quite

> **Quite** comes before `a/an + noun` to mean **"a little / a lot, but not completely"**
> Structure: `quite + a/an + noun`

> *"The new archiving system was **quite a success**."*
> *"It was **quite a** challenge."*

> ⚠️ `quite` ≠ `very` in all contexts. Before a noun phrase → `quite a` (not `quite the`).

---

## 📂 Group 4 — Emphasizing Adverbs in Comparisons

| Comparison Type | Adverbs Used | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Equality** (as...as) | `nearly`, `almost`, `just` | *She's **just as** intelligent as her siblings.* |
| **Comparative** (-er / more...) | `much`, `even`, `still`, `far`, `a lot`, `by far` | *There is a **much better** store around the corner.* |
| **Superlative** (most...) | `by far`, `quite` | *He is **by far the most** forceful speaker.* |

---

## 🎯 Common TOEIC Traps

- **Trap 1 — `well` vs `very`:** Before a prepositional phrase → `well` ✅ (not `very`). *"well below market value"* ✅ / *"very below market value"* ❌
- **Trap 2 — `only` position:** `only` must come **directly before** what it restricts. Misplacing it changes the meaning.
- **Trap 3 — `even` in comparatives:** "a **even** more aggressive approach" → ❌ → "an **even** more aggressive approach" ✅ (note the article)
- **Trap 4 — `much` with superlatives:** `much` is used with comparatives, not superlatives. For superlatives → use `by far`.

---

## 🧠 Exam Thinking Strategy

> **Step 1:** Identify what structure comes **after** the blank.
> **Step 2 Pattern Check:**
> - Blank + prep phrase (`over/below/above`) → **well**
> - Blank + `a/an + noun` → **quite**
> - Blank + comparative adjective (`better`, `more...`) → **much / even / far**
> - Blank + superlative (`the most...`) → **by far**
> - Blank restricts to one option only → **only**
> **Step 3:** If the blank before a noun phrase means "nothing but" → **just / only**

## 📝 Practice Questions

### Q1
> *"This business visa allows you to travel to your destination on the days listed at the bottom of the paper _____."*

- (A) also
- **(B) only ✅**
- (C) however
- (D) still

> 💡 "allowed **only** on the listed days" = exclusively those days. `only` restricts the permission. It modifies the prepositional phrase `on the days listed`.

---

### Q2
> *"Parmalac sold its ImClone stocks at a price that was _____ below the market value at that time."*

- (A) very
- (B) so
- **(C) well ✅**
- (D) such

> 💡 `well below` = significantly below (fixed collocation with prepositional phrase). `very below` is not idiomatic English.

---

### Q3
> *"The company decided that a more aggressive approach to advertising was needed to pull ahead of the competition."*
> *(Blank before "more aggressive")*

- **(A) even ✅**
- (B) very
- (C) really
- (D) much

> 💡 "**even** more aggressive" = shows the approach needs to be **more than before** (surprise/escalation). `much more` also works grammatically, but `even` adds the nuance of *going further than already done*. In context of competition, `even` is the intended emphasis.

## 🗂️ Error Log / Memory Tips

| Mistake | Fix | Memory Trick |
|---|---|---|
| `very below/over` | `well below/over` | `well` = collocates with directional prepositions |
| `much` with superlative | `by far` with superlative | `much better` ✅ / `by far the best` ✅ |
| `quite` without `a/an` | `quite a + noun` | "quite **a** success" — always needs an article |
| `even` position | Between modal and main verb | *We will **even** provide...* |

> ✏️ **Pattern Summary:**
> - `well` + over/below/above/within/beyond
> - `quite` + a/an + noun
> - `much/even/far` + comparative
> - `by far` + the + superlative
> - `only/just` + restricts what follows
