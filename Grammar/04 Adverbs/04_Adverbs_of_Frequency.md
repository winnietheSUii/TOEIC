# 📌 Adverbs of Frequency
**TOEIC Grammar | Unit 04 — Adverbs | Part 4 of 8**
*Level: B1–B2 | Focus: How Often + Double Negative Trap*

---

## 🧭 Overview

> **Adverbs of frequency** answer the question: *"How often?"*
> TOEIC tests their **position in a sentence** and whether you understand their **built-in negative meaning**.

---

## 📊 Frequency Scale — High to Low

| Adverb | Approximate Frequency |
|---|---|
| **always** | 100% |
| **almost always** | ~95% |
| **usually** | ~80% |
| **often / frequently** | ~70% |
| **sometimes** | ~50% |
| **occasionally** | ~30% |
| **seldom / rarely** | ~10% |
| **hardly / scarcely / barely** | ~5% |
| **never** | 0% |

---

## 📍 Position of Adverbs of Frequency

| Position | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Before ordinary verb** | Comes before the main verb | *She **often** tries to help.* |
| **After verb "be"** | Comes after `is/are/was/were` | *He is **usually** very cheerful.* |
| **Between auxiliary + main verb** | Slots between helping verb and main verb | *She can **hardly** keep awake.* |
| **Beginning of sentence** | Can open the sentence (especially `sometimes`) | ***Sometimes** I go to work by bus.* |
| **End of sentence** | Some can appear at the end | *The board meeting takes place **once a year**.* |

---

## 🔢 Frequency with Numbers

> Pattern: `once / twice / three times + a + [time period]`

| Expression | Meaning |
|---|---|
| once a day | every day, one time |
| twice a week | two times per week |
| three times a month | three times per month |
| once a year | annually |

---

## ⚠️ The Double Negative Rule — Critical for TOEIC

> The following adverbs **already contain a negative meaning**:
> `hardly` · `barely` · `scarcely` · `rarely` · `seldom` · `never`

> ❌ **DO NOT** combine them with `not` or `never` — this creates a double negative.

| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| *I had hardly **never** taken lunch outside.* | *I had hardly **ever** taken lunch outside.* |
| *She rarely doesn't come on time.* | *She rarely comes on time.* |
| *He never didn't finish his work.* | *He never finished his work.* |

> ✅ Correct pairings: `hardly ever` · `rarely ever` · `seldom ever`

---

## 🎯 Common TOEIC Traps

- **Trap 1 — Double negative:** `hardly never` ❌ → `hardly ever` ✅ (built-in negative + negative = incorrect)
- **Trap 2 — `ever` in positive adverb slot:** "at the end of **ever** three-month quarter" → `ever` here means *each/any* → ✅ (don't confuse with time adverb `ever`)
- **Trap 3 — `never` at start of sentence:** When `Never` starts a sentence → **inverted word order** follows: *"Never have I seen..."* ✅
- **Trap 4 — `seldom` vs `frequently`:** Context meaning — "to prevent laziness from too much routine" → need to change often → `frequently` ✅

---

## 🧠 Exam Thinking Strategy

> **Step 1:** Read the full sentence for meaning — how often does this happen?
> **Step 2:** Check: is the blank before or after `be`? Before or after the main verb?
> **Step 3:** If blank is a negative frequency adverb (hardly/rarely/seldom) → make sure there's no other negative word nearby.
> **Step 4:** For the *never + inversion* pattern → the subject comes after the auxiliary verb.

## 📝 Practice Questions

### Q1
> *"...evaluations of team leaders are done at the end of _____ three-month quarter."*

- (A) never
- (B) yet
- **(C) ever ✅**
- (D) daily

> 💡 "at the end of **ever** three-month quarter" = at the end of **each/any** quarter. `ever` here functions as a universal quantifier modifying the noun phrase. `never` = wrong meaning; `yet` = time adverb; `daily` = doesn't fit "three-month."

---

### Q2
> *"We have the production laborers change schedules _____ to prevent laziness from too much routine."*

- (A) yet
- (B) ever
- **(C) frequently ✅**
- (D) seldom

> 💡 Purpose is to **prevent** routine by changing schedules → change them **often** → `frequently`. `seldom` (rarely) would mean they barely change = contradicts the goal.

---

### Q3
> *"_____ have I seen such an eager and enthusiastic recruit with so much experience in the workforce."*

- **(A) Never ✅**
- (B) Hardly
- (C) Rarely
- (D) Almost

> 💡 "**Never** have I seen..." = subject-verb inversion after a negative adverb at the start = correct emphatic structure. `Hardly` and `Rarely` can also trigger inversion, but the meaning here requires **never** for the emphatic tone. Check: only `Never`, `Hardly`, `Rarely` can trigger inversion — `Almost` cannot.

## 🗂️ Error Log / Memory Tips

| Mistake | Fix | Memory Trick |
|---|---|---|
| `hardly never` | `hardly ever` | `hardly` = already negative → use `ever` |
| `seldom` in positive context | Check meaning | `seldom` = rarely → only when frequency is VERY low |
| `Never` at sentence start → wrong word order | Invert subject/verb | *Never **have I** seen...* (not *Never I have seen*) |
| `frequently` vs `seldom` | Read for meaning | "prevent routine" = change often = `frequently` |

> ✏️ **Double Negative Cheat Sheet:**
> Negative adverbs: `hardly` / `barely` / `scarcely` / `rarely` / `seldom` / `never`
> → Always pair with **`ever`** (not `never`) in questions/negatives
> → Pattern: `hardly ever` ✅ | `hardly never` ❌
