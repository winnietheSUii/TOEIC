# 📌 Confusing Adverbs
**TOEIC Grammar | Unit 04 — Adverbs | Part 2 of 8**
*Level: B1–B2 | Focus: Same Root, Different Meaning*

---

## 🧭 Overview

> Some adverbs look almost identical but have **completely different meanings**.
> These are classic TOEIC traps — you must know both the form AND the meaning to choose correctly.

---

## 🔄 Confusing Adverb Pairs — Full Reference

### 1️⃣ Hard vs. Hardly

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **hard** | with great effort; intensely | *Employees that work **hard** will be rewarded.* |
| **hardly** | almost not; scarcely | *That was **hardly** enough time for lunch.* |

> ⚠️ `hardly` = **negative meaning** → don't pair with another negative word!

---

### 2️⃣ High vs. Highly

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **high** | at a high physical level | *Her work has piled up **high** on her desk.* |
| **highly** | very much; extremely (with verbs & adjectives) | *I think **highly** of our company's founder.* |

---

### 3️⃣ Great vs. Greatly

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **great** | well / excellently (informal) | *You really have done **great** this year.* |
| **greatly** | to a great extent; very much | *Her writing has **greatly** improved.* |

---

### 4️⃣ Late vs. Lately

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **late** | not on time; toward the end of a period | *The president is scheduled to arrive **late** Friday.* |
| **lately** | recently; in recent times | *Mark has been missing meetings **lately**.* |

---

### 5️⃣ Most vs. Mostly

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **most** | to the greatest degree | *The lack of coordination annoys me **most**.* |
| **mostly** | for the most part; in general | *My co-workers are **mostly** energetic graduates.* |

---

### 6️⃣ Near vs. Nearly

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **near** | close to; approaching (physical/time) | *The final day for registration drew **near**.* |
| **nearly** | almost | *I was **nearly** finished when the phone rang.* |

---

## 📋 Special Note — "Flat Adverbs"

> These words look like adjectives but also function as adverbs **without `-ly`**:

`early` · `late` · `hard` · `high` · `long` · `fast` · `far` · `near` · `daily` · `weekly` · `monthly` · `yearly`

> They serve double duty — same form for both adjective and adverb.
> e.g., *a **fast** car* (adjective) vs. *drive **fast*** (adverb)

---

## 🎯 Common TOEIC Traps

- **Trap 1 — `lately` vs `late`:** "submitted one or two days ___" → action of being not on time → `late` ✅ (not `lately` = recently)
- **Trap 2 — `nearly` vs `near`:** Before a percentage or number → always `nearly` (= almost) → "nearly 15 percent" ✅
- **Trap 3 — `hardly` vs `hard`:** "works ___ diligently" → with effort → `hard` ✅. "worked **hardly**" implies almost no work — opposite meaning!
- **Trap 4 — `highly` vs `high`:** With verbs of opinion/quality (`think`, `recommend`, `regarded`) → always `highly` ✅

---

## 🧠 Exam Thinking Strategy

> **Step 1:** Both options are adverbs — this is a **meaning** question.
> **Step 2:** Check the **sentence meaning** — is it about effort, or scarcity? Proximity, or almost?
> **Step 3:** Check the **sentence structure** — is there already a negative word? (`hardly` + `never` = ❌ double negative)
> **Step 4:** For numbers/percentages → `nearly` (almost). For time expressions → `late` vs `lately` (timing vs. recency).

## 📝 Practice Questions

### Q1
> *"...monthly reports are being submitted one or two days _____."*

- (A) lately
- (B) later
- (C) lateness
- **(D) late ✅**

> 💡 Submitted "not on time" → `late` (not on time). `lately` = recently — wrong meaning here.

---

### Q2
> *"Sales of compact disc players have gone down _____ 15 percent this fiscal year."*

- **(A) nearly ✅**
- (B) near
- (C) quite
- (D) very

> 💡 Before a specific number/percentage → `nearly` (= almost). `near` is a preposition or adjective here, not idiomatic with percentages.

---

### Q3
> *"Kurt Drew works _____, his communication skills are exemplary..."*

- (A) hardly
- (B) harden
- **(C) hard ✅**
- (D) hardened

> 💡 Context: excellent employee, communication praised → works with **great effort** → `hard`. `hardly` would mean he almost doesn't work — contradicts the praise.

## 🗂️ Error Log / Memory Tips

| Pair | Trick to Remember |
|---|---|
| `hard` vs `hardly` | `hardly` = almost NOT → negative flavor |
| `high` vs `highly` | `highly` = with verbs of opinion: *think highly of*, *highly recommended* |
| `late` vs `lately` | `late` = timing; `lately` = like "recently" (has `-ly`, means recently) |
| `near` vs `nearly` | `nearly` = almost → use before numbers/percentages |
| `most` vs `mostly` | `mostly` = "in general / for the most part" |
| `great` vs `greatly` | `greatly` = formal intensifier for verbs (*greatly improved*) |

> ✏️ **Memory Shortcut:**
> - `-ly` pair = usually more **abstract/figurative** meaning
> - Base form (no `-ly`) = usually more **literal/physical** meaning

> ⚠️ **Double Negative Alert:** `hardly`, `rarely`, `seldom`, `barely`, `scarcely`, `never` all carry built-in negation → **never** combine with `not` or `never`.
