# 📌 Quantifiers as Adjectives & Indefinite Pronouns
**TOEIC Grammar | Unit 03 — Adjectives | Part 2 of 4**
*Level: B1–B2 | Focus: Matching Quantifiers to Noun Type*

---

## 🧭 Overview

> Quantifiers express **how many** or **how much**.
> They work as adjectives (before a noun) or as indefinite pronouns (standing alone).
> TOEIC tests whether you can match the right quantifier to the right noun type.

## ❌ Common Error Examples

> *"**Much** customers benefited from the service."*
> ❌ `customers` = plural count → ✅ `**Many** customers`

> *"I was given **few** information at the help desk."*
> ❌ `information` = non-count → ✅ `**little** information`

---

## 📂 Quantifiers as Indefinite Pronouns

> Some quantifiers can stand **alone as pronouns** — often followed by `of the`.

> **Pattern:** `[Quantifier] + of the + [noun]`

> *"**Some of the** progress in sales was due to the unique ads."*
> *"**Most of the** people didn't enjoy the party."*
> *"**None of the** shops were open."*

> Quantifiers that work as pronouns:
> `one / two / each / all / both / none / many / much / most / several / some / any / (a) few / (a) little`

---

## 🎯 Common TOEIC Traps

- **Trap 1 — `every` vs `all`:** `every` + singular noun ✅ | `all` + plural noun ✅. *"Every **order**"* ✅ / *"All **orders**"* ✅
- **Trap 2 — `most` vs `mostly`:** `most` = quantifier (adjective/pronoun). `mostly` = adverb. Cannot swap.
- **Trap 3 — `much` vs `many`:** `much debate` (non-count) ✅ | `many debates` (plural count) ✅
- **Trap 4 — `many` vs `some of many`:** "Pharmacists refused to condone **many** cream" ❌ → cream is non-count → `some` or rephrase

---

## 🧠 Exam Thinking Strategy

> **Step 1:** Identify the noun after the blank — is it **singular count / plural count / non-count**?
> **Step 2:** Use the table above to eliminate incompatible quantifiers immediately.
> **Step 3:** If two quantifiers both fit the noun type → decide by **sentence meaning** (how much? all? some? few?)
> **Step 4:** Is there `of the` after the blank? → Pronoun form needed, not adjective.

## 📝 Practice Questions

### Q1
> *"_____ order that comes to our department is processed with the utmost care and attention."*

- (A) Many · **(B) All... wait — (C) Every ✅** · (D) Most

> 💡 Blank before singular noun `order` → quantifier must work with **singular count**. `Every` ✅ | `All` needs plural → `All orders`. `Many` and `Most` = plural only here.

### Q2
> *"...the cause of _____ debate in the office."*

- **(A) much ✅** · (B) many · (C) mostly · (D) almost

> 💡 `debate` = non-count noun → `much` ✅. `many` = plural count only. `mostly` = adverb (cannot modify noun). `almost` = adverb.

### Q3
> *"Pharmacists have publicly refused to condone _____ cream which claims to provide impossible cosmetic results..."*

- (A) every · (B) a lot · **(C) many... → actually: `every` or check context** · (D) some of

> 💡 `cream` used here as a **count noun** (a specific cream/product) → singular. But the sentence implies multiple creams → `every cream` (each one individually). `many cream` ❌ (count with no -s) — tricky context.

## 🗂️ Error Log / Memory Tips

| Mistake | Fix | Memory Trick |
|---------|-----|--------------|
| `much` + plural noun | `many` + plural | `much` = water, time, money (unmeasurable) |
| `few` + non-count | `little` + non-count | `few` = for things you can count |
| `every` + plural | `every` + singular | `every student` ✅ / `every students` ❌ |
| `most` vs `mostly` | `most` = quantity; `mostly` = adverb | Can you replace it with "for the most part"? → `mostly` |

> ✏️ **Memory Shortcut:**
> - Can you count it? → `many / few / several`
> - Can't count it? → `much / little / a little`
> - Both? → `some / any / more / most / all`
