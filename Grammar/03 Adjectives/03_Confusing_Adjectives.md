# 📌 Confusing Adjectives
**TOEIC Grammar | Unit 03 — Adjectives | Part 3 of 4**
*Level: B1–B2 | Focus: Same Root, Different Meaning*

---

## 🧭 Overview

> Some adjectives are derived from the **same root** but have **distinct meanings**.
> Choosing the wrong one is not a grammar mistake — it's a **meaning mistake**.
> These are pure meaning-based traps. You must know both forms.

---

## 📋 Commonly Confused Adjective Pairs

| Pair | Distinction |
|------|-------------|
| **appreciable** vs **appreciative** | `appreciable` = noticeable/significant · `appreciative` = grateful |
| **argumentative** vs **arguable** | `argumentative` = tends to argue · `arguable` = debatable/questionable |
| **beneficial** vs **beneficent** | `beneficial` = helpful/good for · `beneficent` = doing good deeds (formal) |
| **careful** vs **caring** | `careful` = gives close attention · `caring` = kind/empathetic |
| **considerable** vs **considerate** | `considerable` = large in amount · `considerate` = thoughtful of others |
| **comparable** vs **comparative** | `comparable` = similar/equal in value · `comparative` = involving comparison |
| **comprehensible** vs **comprehensive** | `comprehensible` = easy to understand · `comprehensive` = covering everything |
| **economic** vs **economical** | `economic` = relating to the economy · `economical` = cost-effective/not wasteful |
| **favorable** vs **favorite** | `favorable` = positive/approving · `favorite` = most preferred |
| **impressive** vs **impressed** | `impressive` = causes admiration (thing) · `impressed` = feels admiration (person) |
| **informed** vs **informative** | `informed` = has knowledge · `informative` = provides useful info |
| **persuasive** vs **persuaded** | `persuasive` = able to convince (person/thing) · `persuaded` = has been convinced |
| **profitable** vs **proficient** | `profitable` = makes money · `proficient` = highly skilled |
| **reliable** vs **reliant** | `reliable` = can be trusted · `reliant` = dependent on something |
| **respectable** vs **respective** | `respectable` = deserving respect · `respective` = belonging to each one mentioned |
| **responsible** vs **responsive** | `responsible` = accountable/in charge · `responsive` = reacts quickly |
| **satisfactory** vs **satisfying** | `satisfactory` = meets requirements/standards · `satisfying` = pleasing/fulfilling |
| **seasonal** vs **seasoned** | `seasonal` = related to a season · `seasoned` = experienced/skilled |
| **successful** vs **successive** | `successful` = achieved its goal · `successive` = following one after another |
| **understanding** vs **understandable** | `understanding` = sympathetic · `understandable` = can be understood |

---

## 🔑 The `-ive` vs `-ed` Pattern (Also Applies Here)

> Beyond the pairs above, remember the core rule from Word Forms:
> - **`-ive` / `-ing`** = the thing/person that **causes** a feeling or quality
> - **`-ed`** = the person who **receives or has** a feeling

| Word | Who/What is described |
|------|----------------------|
| `impressive performance` | The performance causes admiration |
| `impressed critic` | The critic feels admiration |
| `satisfying result` | The result causes satisfaction |
| `satisfactory result` | The result meets the required standard |

## ⚠️ Error Examples from Source

> ❌ *"Background music has an **appreciative** effect on productivity."*
> ✅ `appreciable` = noticeable/measurable effect (not "grateful effect")

> ❌ *"It is important to keep your supervisor **informative** about any problems."*
> ✅ `informed` = the supervisor has the information (not the supervisor provides info)

> ❌ *"Representatives are trained not to be **argumentative** with customers."*
> ✅ Wait — actually `argumentative` IS correct here: means "tends to argue." The source correction says `arguable` — but `argumentative` fits "not wanting to argue with customers" better. *Always read full context.*

## 🎯 Common TOEIC Traps

- **Trap 1 — `economic` vs `economical`:** Relating to the economy → `economic policy`. Saving money/not wasteful → `economical solution`
- **Trap 2 — `considerable` vs `considerate`:** Amount/degree → `considerable effort`. Thoughtful of others → `considerate employee`
- **Trap 3 — `comprehensive` vs `comprehensible`:** Covers all topics → `comprehensive guide`. Easy to understand → `comprehensible explanation`
- **Trap 4 — `satisfactory` vs `satisfying`:** Meets minimum standard → `satisfactory result`. Emotionally fulfilling → `satisfying experience`

---

## 🧠 Exam Thinking Strategy

> **Step 1:** Both options are adjectives → this is a **meaning question**, not grammar.
> **Step 2:** Read the full sentence carefully — what quality is being described?
> **Step 3:** Check the noun being modified — is it a person, an action, or a result?
> **Step 4:** Apply the key question: Does it mean "causing" or "receiving/having" the quality?

## 📝 Practice Questions

### Q1
> *"Despite consumer opposition, Noden will spend time researching the _____ benefits of adding GPS to its cell phones."*

- (A) satisfaction · (B) satisfactory · **(C) satisfying ✅** · (D) consideration

> 💡 Modifies `benefits` → adjective. `satisfying benefits` = benefits that feel fulfilling/rewarding to users. `satisfactory` = meets a minimum standard — too weak for "benefits." `satisfaction` = noun.

### Q2
> *"Because his response to questions was less _____ than expected, school officials denied him acceptance..."*

- (A) satisfied · **(B) satisfactory ✅** · (C) satisfying · (D) satisfaction

> 💡 "less _____ than expected" → his response didn't meet the **required standard** → `satisfactory` (= adequate/acceptable). `satisfying` = emotionally pleasing — not the right register for academic evaluation.

### Q3
> *"Stunned by the _____ performance of the violinist, the magazine critic was at a loss for words..."*

- **(A) impressive ✅** · (B) impressed · (C) impression · (D) impressively

> 💡 Before noun `performance` → adjective. The performance **causes** admiration → `-ive` form ✅. `impressed` = the person who feels admiration (wrong noun). `impressively` = adverb.
