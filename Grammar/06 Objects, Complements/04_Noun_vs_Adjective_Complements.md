# 📌 Noun Complements vs. Adjective Complements
**TOEIC Grammar | Unit 07 — Objects & Complements | Part 4 of 4**
*Level: B1–B2 | Focus: Choosing Between Noun and Adjective in Complement Slots*

---

## 🧭 Overview

> Both **nouns** and **adjectives** can fill a complement slot — but they serve different purposes.
> The question is: does the complement **identify** (who/what is it?) or **describe** (what is it like?).
> This is a pure meaning choice — grammar alone won't help you choose between them.

---

## 📂 Noun Complement — Identifies Who or What

> A noun complement gives **identifying information** — it tells us the role, title, or category of the subject or object.
> Pattern: linking verb + **noun phrase**

| Example | Identified as... |
|---------|-----------------|
| *"Alice is **a ballet dancer**."* | Her role/identity |
| *"After two months, Jack became **a general manager**."* | His new position |
| *"People called him **a liar**."* | How others categorize him |
| *"We considered his speech **a masterpiece**."* | Category/evaluation of the speech |

> 💡 Noun complements often use the article `a/an` before them.

---

## 📂 Adjective Complement — Describes a Quality or State

> An adjective complement gives **descriptive or evaluative information** — it describes a quality, feeling, appearance, or condition.
> Pattern: linking verb + **adjective**

| Example | Quality described |
|---------|-----------------|
| *"The paintings are **impressive**."* | quality of the paintings |
| *"She seemed **bothered** at work today."* | her apparent state |
| *"The manager made the workers **nervous**."* | the state of the workers |
| *"The bank keeps its depositors **satisfied**."* | the state of depositors |

---

## 🔑 Decision Rule

| Ask yourself... | Answer → Use |
|----------------|-------------|
| Does the complement say **what/who** it is? (role, title, category) | → **Noun** |
| Does the complement say **what it's like** (quality, state, feeling, evaluation)? | → **Adjective** |

---

## 🎯 Common TOEIC Traps

- **Trap 1 — Noun when adjective is needed:** `"Mrs. Heinz is confidence that..."` ❌ → `confident` ✅ (describes her feeling, not her identity)
- **Trap 2 — Adjective when noun is needed:** `"It was a great disappointed"` ❌ → `disappointment` ✅ (identifies what it was, after `a great`)
- **Trap 3 — Adverb in complement slot:** `"be found to be **confidently**"` ❌ → `confident` ✅ (adverbs cannot be complements)
- **Trap 4 — Verb in complement slot:** `"considered the proposal **feasibility**"` ❌ → `feasible` ✅ (adjective evaluates the proposal)

---

## 🧠 Exam Thinking Strategy

> **Step 1:** Is the blank after a linking verb or after `verb + object`? → Complement slot.
> **Step 2:** Read the surrounding context:
> - Is there `a/an` before the blank? → **Noun** (articles precede nouns)
> - Is there `quite/very/rather/less` before the blank? → **Adjective** (degree adverbs modify adjectives)
> - Is the complement describing a state/feeling? → **Adjective**
> - Is the complement giving a label or category? → **Noun**
> **Step 3:** Eliminate verbs and adverbs first — they never work here.
> **Step 4:** Between noun and adjective → apply the "identify vs. describe" rule.

## 📝 Practice Questions

### Q1
> *"Mrs. Heinz is _____ that the company will be more successful next year."*

- (A) confidence · **(B) confident ✅** · (C) confidently · (D) confide

> 💡 `is _____ that...` → subject complement after `is` + that-clause = evaluative feeling → **adjective** → `confident`. `confidence` = noun (can't precede a that-clause this way); `confidently` = adverb; `confide` = verb.

### Q2
> *"The new equipment was found to be quite _____ for the company's needs."*

- **(A) suitable ✅** · (B) suitability · (C) suit · (D) suitably

> 💡 `found to be quite _____` → object complement + `quite` (degree adverb) → **adjective** → `suitable`. `quite` modifies adjectives, not nouns or adverbs; `suit` = verb; `suitably` = adverb.

### Q3
> *"The committee considered the proposal _____ and approved it without further discussion."*

- **(A) feasible ✅** · (B) feasibility · (C) feasibly · (D) feasible *(same)*

> 💡 `considered the proposal _____` → object complement after `consider + object` → evaluates the proposal's quality → **adjective** → `feasible`. `feasibility` = noun (identifies "what"), but context is an evaluation of quality → adjective wins. `feasibly` = adverb ❌.

## 🗂️ Error Log / Memory Tips

| Signal Before Blank | Required Complement | Example |
|--------------------|--------------------|---------| 
| `a / an` | Noun | *"became **a manager**"* |
| `quite / very / rather` | Adjective | *"found it **quite suitable**"* |
| State/feeling verb | Adjective | *"seemed **bothered**"* |
| Role/identity verb | Noun | *"called him **a leader**"* |
| `considered/found` + evaluation | Adjective | *"found the proposal **feasible**"* |

> ✏️ **Cheat Questions:**
> - Can you replace the blank with "a/an + noun"? → It's a noun complement slot.
> - Can you replace the blank with "very + adjective"? → It's an adjective complement slot.
