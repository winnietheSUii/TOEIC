# 📌 Adjective Forms
**TOEIC Grammar | Unit 05 — Word Forms | Part 3 of 5**
*Level: B1–B2 | Focus: Adjective Suffixes, Participial Adjectives & Position*

---

## 🧭 Overview

> Adjectives **describe nouns** and can appear in two positions:
> 1. **Before a noun** → "a *successful* campaign"
> 2. **After a linking verb** → "The campaign was *successful*"
>
> TOEIC tests whether you can identify an adjective slot, choose the right adjective suffix, and avoid confusing adjectives with adverbs or nouns.

---

## 📍 Two Adjective Positions

| Position | Signal | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Attributive** (before noun) | Blank is before a noun | *a **reliable** source* |
| **Predicative** (after linking verb) | `is/are/was/were/seem/become/remain` + blank | *The results were **positive**.* |

> ⚠️ Adverbs **cannot** fill these positions. Only adjectives describe nouns.

## 📋 Adjective Suffixes — Grouped by Type

### ➕ Positive Qualities

| Suffix | Examples |
|---|---|
| `-ful` | successful, helpful, meaningful, resourceful |
| `-ive` | productive, effective, innovative, competitive, attentive |
| `-able / -ible` | reliable, available, flexible, responsible, accessible |
| `-ous` | advantageous, numerous, various, prestigious, cautious |
| `-ent / -ant` | efficient, consistent, significant, relevant, compliant |
| `-ic` | systematic, economic, strategic, realistic, dynamic |

### ➖ Negative / Absence Qualities

| Suffix | Examples |
|---|---|
| `-less` | effortless, careless, seamless, countless, worthless |
| `un-` (prefix) | unnecessary, unavailable, unsuccessful, unexpected |
| `in- / im-` (prefix) | incomplete, inefficient, inaccessible, impractical |

## 🔄 Participial Adjectives — `-ed` vs `-ing`

> These come from verbs and function as adjectives — one of the trickiest areas in TOEIC!

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **-ed** (past participle) | The noun **receives** the feeling/action | *a **satisfied** customer*, *an **experienced** manager* |
| **-ing** (present participle) | The noun **causes** the feeling/action | *a **satisfying** experience*, *a **challenging** task* |

### Decision Rule:

> Ask: Does the noun **cause** or **receive** the feeling?
> - **Receives** the feeling → `-ed`
> - **Causes** the feeling → `-ing`

| Example | Analysis |
|---|---|
| *a **motivated** employee* | The employee **receives** motivation → `-ed` ✅ |
| *a **motivating** speech* | The speech **causes** motivation → `-ing` ✅ |
| *an **experienced** consultant* | The consultant **has** experience (receives/has) → `-ed` ✅ |
| *an **exhausting** project* | The project **causes** exhaustion → `-ing` ✅ |
| *an **exhausted** worker* | The worker **feels** exhausted (receives) → `-ed` ✅ |

## 🎯 Common TOEIC Traps

- **Trap 1 — Adverb in adjective slot:** "a **professionally** team" ❌ → "a **professional** team" ✅
- **Trap 2 — Noun in adjective slot:** "a **profession** team" ❌ → "a **professional** team" ✅
- **Trap 3 — `-ed` vs `-ing` adjective:** "a **challenging** candidate" ❌ (candidate doesn't cause challenge) → "a **challenged** candidate" or better → "a **qualified** candidate"
- **Trap 4 — `-ly` adjective mistaken for adverb:** `timely`, `friendly`, `costly`, `quarterly` are **adjectives**, not adverbs

---

## 🧠 Exam Thinking Strategy

> **Step 1:** Is the blank before a noun or after a linking verb? → **Adjective** needed.
> **Step 2:** Choose the right suffix — positive/negative quality, formal category, or participial?
> **Step 3:** For `-ed` vs `-ing`:
> - "Does the noun **feel** something or **cause** something?"
> - Feels/receives → **-ed** | Causes/produces → **-ing**
> **Step 4:** Watch out for `-ly` words: they may be **adjectives** (timely, friendly) not adverbs!

## 📝 Practice Questions

### Q1
> *"The company is looking for a _____ candidate with at least five years of experience in finance."*

- (A) qualify
- (B) qualification
- (C) qualified ✅
- (D) qualifying

> 💡 Before noun `candidate` → **adjective**. The candidate **has** qualifications (received/has) → `-ed` form. `qualifying` = causing qualification (like "qualifying round") — doesn't describe a person.

---

### Q2
> *"The presentation of the quarterly results was _____ clear and easy to understand."*

- (A) exception
- (B) exceptional
- (C) exceptionally ✅
- (D) except

> 💡 Blank modifies two adjectives (`clear` and `easy`) → **adverb** needed. `exceptionally` (`-ly`) ✅. `exceptional` = adjective, cannot modify another adjective.

*(Note: This tests adverb vs adjective — a critical distinction.)*

---

### Q3
> *"We received _____ feedback from all departments regarding the new system."*

- (A) encourage
- (B) encouraging ✅
- (C) encouraged
- (D) encouragement

> 💡 Before noun `feedback` → **adjective**. The feedback **causes** encouragement (it makes people feel encouraged) → `-ing` ✅. `encouraged feedback` would mean the feedback itself felt encouraged — illogical.

## 🗂️ Error Log / Memory Tips

| Mistake | Fix | Memory Trick |
|---|---|---|
| `-ly` word chosen as adjective | Check: is it before a noun? | `timely reminder` (adj) vs `surprisingly good` (adv) |
| `-ed` vs `-ing` confusion | Ask: causes or receives? | Person → `-ed`; Thing that causes feeling → `-ing` |
| Noun suffix in adjective slot | Check: `-al/-ive/-ous` = adj | "**financial** report" not "**finance** report" |
| Adverb chosen for before-noun slot | Only adjectives go before nouns | "a **successful** campaign" not "a **successfully** campaign" |

> ✏️ **Participial Adjective Cheat:**
> - 😊 *"I feel **satisfied**"* → the feeling **is in** me ← `-ed`
> - 🌟 *"This job is **satisfying**"* → the job **creates** the feeling ← `-ing`
