# 04_Prepositions_of_Direction

## 📌 Overview
Prepositions of direction show **movement** from one place to another.
Like place prepositions, many direction prepositions have **extended / figurative meanings** that appear heavily in TOEIC Part 5.

The most high-yield TOEIC trap: **"out of" fixed expressions** (out of stock, out of order, etc.)

---

## 📊 Reference Table — BASIC DIRECTION

| Preposition | Meaning | Example |
|-------------|---------|---------|
| **from** | point of origin / source | obtained from the office |
| **to** | destination / target | send the package to the client |
| **toward(s)** | in the direction of (not necessarily reaching) | walked toward the exit |
| **into** | movement entering something | moved into the room |
| **out of** | movement exiting something | took the key out of the pocket |
| **through** | movement passing inside/across | walked through the woods |
| **across** | movement from one side to the other | walked across the bridge |
| **along** | movement following a line/path | walked along the street |
| **for** | destination (especially departing) | leaving for Busan |

## 🧠 Core Rules

### Rule 1 — "out of" = fixed expression trigger
> When you see **"out of ___"**, it's almost always a fixed expression.
> Check the fixed expression list — do NOT try to analyze direction meaning.
```
✅ "The product is out of stock." (fixed = no items)
✅ "The elevator is out of order." (fixed = broken)
❌ "The product is without stock." (wrong collocation)
```

### Rule 2 — "to" vs "toward"
```
TO      = reaches the destination
TOWARD  = moves in the direction of (may not arrive)

✅ "She walked to the office." (she arrived)
✅ "She walked toward the office." (she was heading there)
```

### Rule 3 — "through" vs "across"
```
THROUGH = movement that goes INSIDE something (3D path)
ACROSS  = movement from one side to the other (2D surface)

✅ "walked through the tunnel" (inside it)
✅ "walked across the bridge" (surface, side to side)
```

### Rule 4 — "from" vs "of"
> In TOEIC passive structures, **"by"** is standard, but **"from"** can appear with some verbs:
```
distinguish A from B       (not "from A of B")
remove ___ from the list   (not "remove ___ of")
obtained from              (source)
```

---

## ⚠️ TOEIC Traps

| Trap | Wrong | Correct |
|------|-------|---------|
| "out of" fixed | without stock | **out of** stock |
| "out of" fixed | in order (machine) | **out of** order |
| Destination prep | leaving to Busan | leaving **for** Busan |
| Throughout meaning | through the country | **across** the country |
| Direction vs source | removed to the list | removed **from** the list |

---

## 🎯 Exam Thinking Strategy
1. **Is "out of" in the choices?** → check fixed expression list immediately
2. **Is it a departure destination?** → use **for** (leaving for, departing for, headed for)
3. **Is it passing inside (3D)?** → **through** | Is it side-to-side (2D surface)?→ **across**
4. **Is "to" followed by a feeling/reaction noun?** → fixed expression (to my relief, to my knowledge)
5. **Does the verb need "from"?** → distinguish / remove / obtain / separate → always "from"

## ✏️ Practice Questions

**Q1.** Subscribers were informed that their names would be removed ___ the list if they did not reply to the e-mail.
- (A) to
- (B) **from** ✅
- (C) for
- (D) of

> **Why (B):** "removed **from** the list" = taken off. The verb "remove" always pairs with **from** to show the source being separated.
> (A) "removed to" = moved to a location, not taken off a list. (C) "removed for" = removed for a reason (different meaning). (D) "removed of" = not standard.

---

**Q2.** The new highway, which will run ___ the mountains, is expected to cause damage to wildlife.
- (A) **through** ✅
- (B) during
- (C) out
- (D) against

> **Why (A):** "run **through** the mountains" = the highway passes inside/across the mountain area (3D path).
> (B) "during" is a time preposition, not direction. (C) "out" alone is incomplete — needs "out of." (D) "against" means in opposition to, not a path.

---

**Q3.** This product should be kept ___ reach of children as it contains toxic agents.
- (A) away with
- (B) **out of** ✅
- (C) up until
- (D) without

> **Why (B):** "**out of reach**" = fixed expression meaning "in a place that cannot be accessed."
> (A) "away with" is not a standard fixed expression here. (C) "up until" is a time expression. (D) "without reach" is not a standard phrase.
