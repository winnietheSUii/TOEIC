# 02_Prepositions_of_Time

## 📌 Overview
Prepositions of time in TOEIC fall into two groups:
1. **Point of time** — when exactly something happens (since, by, before, until)
2. **Duration / period** — how long something lasts (for, during, over, within, throughout)

Choosing between these is a Part 5 top-5 trap.

---

## 📊 Reference Table — POINT OF TIME

| Preposition | Meaning | Key Pattern | Example |
|-------------|---------|-------------|---------|
| **since** | from a past point up to now | since + point (used with perfect tense) | since 5:00 p.m., since 2010 |
| **from … to** | start point to end point | from X to Y | from Monday to Friday |
| **until / till** | continuing up to a point | until + deadline | work until the deadline |
| **by** | no later than a point | by + deadline (action must finish) | submit by Friday |
| **before** | earlier than a point | before + event | before the meeting |
| **prior to** | before (formal) | prior to + noun | prior to the announcement |
| **after** | later than a point | after + event | after the conference |
| **following** | right after (formal) | following + noun | following the merger |
| **upon** | immediately after | upon + noun/-ing | upon arrival, upon receiving |

---

## 📊 Reference Table — DURATION / PERIOD

| Preposition | Meaning | Key Pattern | Example |
|-------------|---------|-------------|---------|
| **for** | how long (measured duration) | for + number + time unit | for ten years, for two weeks |
| **during** | within a named period | during + named period | during the conference |
| **over** | throughout a long period | over + period | over the past decade |
| **throughout** | during the entire period | throughout + period | throughout the year |
| **within** | before the end of a period | within + period | within two weeks |

## 🧠 Core Rules

### Rule 1 — by vs until
```
BY     = deadline for COMPLETION → action must be DONE by this point
UNTIL  = action CONTINUES up to this point

✅ "Submit the report by Friday." (must finish by then)
✅ "Work continues until Friday." (still ongoing, stops at Friday)
❌ "Submit the report until Friday." (wrong — "until" = duration, not deadline)
```

### Rule 2 — for vs during
```
FOR    = measured duration (number + time unit)
DURING = named period (event/period name)

✅ "She worked there for five years."
✅ "She worked there during the summer."
❌ "She worked there during five years." (wrong — "during" needs a named period)
❌ "She worked there for the summer." (wrong — "for" needs a measured unit)
```

### Rule 3 — within vs by
```
WITHIN = inside a period (flexible, before the period ends)
BY     = at the latest (a strict deadline point)

✅ "Submit within a month." (anytime in the next month)
✅ "Submit by May 1." (must be done on/before May 1)
```

### Rule 4 — since vs for
```
SINCE = from a POINT of time → used with PRESENT PERFECT
FOR   = for a DURATION       → used with PRESENT PERFECT or other tenses

✅ "She has worked here since 2010."  (point = 2010)
✅ "She has worked here for 14 years." (duration = 14 years)
```

## ⚠️ TOEIC Traps

| Trap | Wrong | Correct |
|------|-------|---------|
| Deadline confusion | "complete until Friday" | complete **by** Friday |
| Named period | "during two weeks" | **for** two weeks |
| Measured duration | "for the conference" | **during** the conference |
| Point vs duration | "since two years" | **for** two years |
| Formal "before" | "before the announcement" (informal OK) | **prior to** the announcement (formal/TOEIC) |
| Formal "after" | "after the merger" | **following** the merger (formal) |
| Immediate "after" | "after receiving" | **upon** receiving (TOEIC formal) |

---

## 🎯 Exam Thinking Strategy
1. **Is it a point or deadline?** → by / before / prior to / until
2. **Is it a measured period?** → for + number
3. **Is it a named period?** → during / throughout / over
4. **Is it "must finish before X"?** → **by** (not until)
5. **Is it formal context?** → prefer: prior to / following / upon / throughout

## ✏️ Practice Questions

**Q1.** Because fewer than five people have registered for the workshop to be held ___ two weeks now, the training manager has decided to cancel.
- (A) from
- (B) **within** ✅
- (C) for
- (D) until

> **Why (B):** "within two weeks" = before the end of the two-week period — the workshop is scheduled inside that window.
> (A) "from" needs a paired "to." (C) "for" indicates duration of action, not a scheduled window. (D) "until" = continuing up to a point, not a scheduled event.

---

**Q2.** The rental deposit must be transferred to the landlord's bank account at least fifteen days ___ the moving-in date.
- (A) from
- (B) within
- (C) until
- (D) **by** ✅

> **Why (D):** "by the moving-in date" = the deposit must be completed no later than that date — it's a deadline.
> (A) "from" shows a starting point, not a deadline. (B) "within fifteen days" would work but "within the moving-in date" is unnatural. (C) "until" = continuous action, not a completion deadline.

---

**Q3.** To have broken parts replaced without charge, the registration form must be mailed to the company one month ___ product purchase.
- (A) after
- (B) **within** ✅
- (C) until
- (D) beyond

> **Why (B):** "within one month of purchase" = must be done before the one-month window closes.
> (A) "after one month" = after the period ends (too late). (C) "until" = continuing, not a deadline window. (D) "beyond" = past/exceeding, wrong direction.
