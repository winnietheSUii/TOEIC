# 06_Preposition_Of_and_Others

## 📌 Overview
This unit covers two key areas:
1. **"A of B" structure** — the most common preposition structure in English
2. **Topic/Subject prepositions** — prepositions used to draw attention to a topic (about, regarding, concerning, with regard to)

Both are extremely high-frequency in TOEIC Part 5 and Part 6.

---

## 📊 Reference Table — "A of B" Patterns

| Pattern | Relationship | Examples |
|---------|-------------|---------|
| **A = noun, B = implied subject** | B performs the action of A | the development **of** the company, the consent **of** the parent, the retirement **of** our colleague |
| **A = noun, B = implied object** | B receives the action of A | the showing **of** his movie, the marketing **of** luxury brands, the shipment **of** wine |
| **A = B (appositives)** | A and B refer to the same thing | a chance **of** meeting someone, the idea **of** producing handbooks, a balance **of** 100 dollars |
| **A is part of B** | A belongs to / is inside B | this area **of** the city, the end **of** the month, the employees **of** the branch |

> ⚡ **"of" is the default possession/relationship preposition** — when you need to link two nouns, "of" is almost always correct.

---

## 📊 Reference Table — TOPIC / SUBJECT PREPOSITIONS

| Preposition | Formality | Example |
|-------------|----------|---------|
| **about** | casual | a question about the schedule |
| **on** | neutral — specific topic | a discussion on ethics, a book on management |
| **over** | neutral — debate/dispute | a dispute over workers' rights |
| **as for / as to** | neutral — introducing topic | As for the budget, we need approval. |
| **concerning** | formal | questions concerning the internship |
| **regarding** | formal | a notice regarding policy changes |
| **with/in regard to** | very formal | with regard to food safety |
| **with respect to** | very formal | with respect to your request |
| **with/in reference to** | very formal | in reference to your letter |

## 🧠 Core Rules

### Rule 1 — "about" vs "on" vs "over"
```
ABOUT = general topic (any context)
ON    = specific/focused topic (presentations, books, discussions)
OVER  = contested/debated topic (disputes, arguments, disagreements)

✅ "a book on leadership"     (focused topic)
✅ "a question about the plan" (general topic)
✅ "a dispute over territory"  (contested topic)
❌ "a dispute about territory"  (works but less precise — "over" is preferred)
```

### Rule 2 — Formal replacements (TOEIC loves formal)
```
CASUAL:   "about your request"
FORMAL:   "regarding your request"
          "concerning your request"
          "with regard to your request"
          "with respect to your request"
          "in reference to your request"

→ All mean the same thing — TOEIC prefers the formal versions
```

### Rule 3 — "A of B" is the default
```
When connecting two nouns where one owns/contains/causes the other:
✅ "the end of the month" (part of)
✅ "the development of the company" (subject of)
✅ "the shipment of goods" (object of)
```

---

## ⚠️ TOEIC Traps

| Trap | Wrong | Correct |
|------|-------|---------|
| "regarding to" | regarding to the plan | **regarding** the plan (no "to") |
| "as for" vs "as to" | Both correct | both work — "as for" = more common |
| dispute + about | dispute about wages | dispute **over** wages (preferred) |
| "on" for specific topic | discussion about ethics | discussion **on** ethics (specific focus) |
| double "of" | the audit of the company, the end of the month | ✅ Both correct — "of" can repeat |

---

## 🎯 Exam Thinking Strategy
1. **Two nouns need connecting?** → likely "of" (the ___ of the ___)
2. **Blank before a topic?** → check formality: about (casual) / regarding (formal) / with regard to (very formal)
3. **Debate or dispute?** → **over** (not "about")
4. **Book / report / presentation?** → **on** (specific topic)
5. **"With ___ to" pattern?** → regard / respect / reference

## ✏️ Practice Questions

**Q1.** The independent audit ___ the company is expected to be concluded by the end ___ this month.
- (A) **of, of** ✅
- (B) by, of
- (C) on, at
- (D) with, by

> **Why (A):** Both blanks use "of" — "the audit **of** the company" (A of B = B is the object being audited) and "the end **of** this month" (A of B = A is part of B).
> (B) "held by" is possible but "audit of" is the standard collocation. (C) "on, at" doesn't fit either position. (D) "with, by" is incorrect for both.

---

**Q2.** Dr. Wilson will attend the workshop to moderate a panel discussion ___ ethics of new medical technology.
- (A) by
- (B) to
- (C) **on** ✅
- (D) with

> **Why (C):** "a discussion **on** ethics" = a discussion focused on a specific topic. "On" is used for specific, focused topics in academic/professional contexts.
> (A) "by" shows agent, not topic. (B) "to" shows direction. (D) "with" shows accompaniment, not topic.

---

**Q3.** A dispute ___ workers' rights has caused union members to walk out in a week-long strike.
- (A) **over** ✅
- (B) above
- (C) for
- (D) of

> **Why (A):** "a dispute **over** workers' rights" = a debate/argument about a contested topic. "Over" is the standard preposition for disputes, debates, and disagreements.
> (B) "above" = higher than (physical position). (C) "for" = purpose/benefit. (D) "of" = possession/relationship, not suitable for disputes.
